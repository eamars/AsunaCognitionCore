# Asuna Runtime API

The business contract between the worker, the Host plugin, channel adapters and the brains' tools. See [NATIVE_PLUGIN.md](NATIVE_PLUGIN.md) for composition and [RUN_ASUNA.md](RUN_ASUNA.md) for installation. DSH owns model execution and Web sessions; its managed Python worker serves the normalized channel API below.

## Runtime responsibilities

`RuntimeHost` inside the business worker owns Application, the queue/action `Chat` controller, memory indexing and summaries, and configured channels/integrations. The Core Host plugin owns actual native agents and the DSH schedule service. Native composer input enters through SessionController and is associated with persisted input by native message ID; the Web UI never reads a parallel Asuna chat store.

Inbound work is persisted before retrieval or model calls. Character turns and action tasks use separate queues. The host binds each turn and task to a scene, person, policy epoch, and capability set. A new context changes the DSH conversation context while retaining persisted scene memory.

A character turn is one native DSH turn. She first writes a short thought with `think` (she is asked for at most 300 characters; a longer one is kept and only its own result says how many characters over; it is kept as a monologue memory and her last few thoughts in the scene reach her next turn; a past thought she gets back, recalled or recent, says when she had it (a recent one by its clock stamp, a recalled one also how long ago) and what she did then, `reasons.then`: her words or her silence's reason as written, and her actions with consequences in plain first-person words, one per line; the Host leaves out a thought whose `think` call her session still shows (anywhere after its last compaction checkpoint), as it does for memories, blocks and history rows; within one notice a line is sent once, in `delivered_history` first (a group's thread lines and her recent experience leave it out, and a task's original input that is such a line is named by `original_input_id`), and a block whose lists only changed order counts as unchanged; and when a compaction later takes a copy a notice relied on, the next step's start sends it again in a follow-up notice (`restored_from_program`), `context-delivery.js`; each call with consequences — the calls `reasons._action` words, the one table the night block also counts by — records her newest thought in the turn as `reason_ref`, in the episode's `tool_calls` and the `role_tool` audit, and that thought comes back as she wrote it, as `your_thought_then`, with what the call made: a firing plan's `scheduled_plan_from_program`, a task's result, the night block where she wrote no reason, and, as a `> （你说那句时心里想的：…）` line, a line that quotes one of hers, in a group or a private chat, given as its first sentences within about 60 characters with `…（截断，原文 N 字）` when cut; the whole thought stays where `recall` finds it). She then uses her other tools: `recall`; her own records (`write_document`, `understand_person`, `set_policy`, `feel`, `promote_memory`); `plan`; `attach_image`; `group_action`; and `delegate` and `task` (`do` message, answer, stop, page) to work with the action brain; her improvement-idea notebook is `ideas` (`op` note, read, review). A tool with options is listed and allowed per option (`role_tools.OPS`; a turn may allow some of its options), and an option a turn may not use is refused in words saying when it can be. The names `message_action`, `answer_action`, `stop_action`, `read_report`, `note_idea`, `read_ideas` and `review_idea`, and `pass_note` and `leave_note`, are registered but never listed: a call by one runs as its option (the last two as `note`) and its result says so (`renamed`); records keep the tool, its `op` and `called_as`. `message_developer` is registered the same way with its own parameters (`level`, `text`): it runs as `ask` to the developer's line, `wake` with the patience 一小时 and `note` with 回了为止. A conversation lists the same tools in every turn (`role_tools.toolbox`), so the system prompt and the tool list at the start of each request stay unchanged: its place decides them — everywhere her own mind's tools and, where anyone may hand work over, the action tools; in her home conversations (the owner's local chat and private chats, and the trusted lines; a group in `home_groups` reads as home but keeps a group's tools) `write_document`, `note`, `set_policy`, `ideas` read and review, `ask` (`do` ask, read, close), `restart`, `place_timezone`, `group_focus` and `peer_line`; in groups `write_document`, `note`, `await_answer`, `quote`, `find_member`, `group_focus` and, where she is an admin, `group_action`; elsewhere `note`; `watch` on platform conversations; `read_image` where the channel carries pictures and her route sees them, `attach_image` where pictures can be sent, `sticker` where stickers can be sent and in the local chat; `errand` in the owner's own conversations; `visit`, `promote_memory` and `credential` in the local chat. The turn decides which of them it may use (`role_tools.exposed`, recorded as the episode's `turn_tools`): a call to a listed tool the turn may not use is refused with when it can be used (`role_tools.NOT_NOW`). A refused call is a tool error she reads and can correct in the same turn; it never fails the turn. `feel` without `do` but with a `feeling` records; `plan` without `do` or `plan_id` but with an intent and a timing creates, and its result says so; `update` or `cancel` reaches any plan of this conversation still active, listed or not, and one that already ran or was cancelled is refused with that reason. A free-text parameter the program refuses past a length states it in its description (`_s(limit=…)`; DSH's tool schema subset has no `maxLength`); copied identifiers and safety caps of 20,000 characters or more are left out. A text over its length is refused and nothing is done; the refusal offers its beginning cut to fit, at the last sentence end that fits (else at the limit), ending with the mark （截在这里，原文还有 N 字） (`config.cut`); so do a note's and a plan intent's. A parameter is shown by one name (`param_names.RENAMED` maps it to the name the handler reads); a call of hers written with the old name is refused by name (`param_names.GONE`), except the old names in `param_names.STILL_TAKEN` (`plan`'s `op`), which run and say the new name. The action brain's calls keep taking the old names. Action tool arguments pass `tool_args.normalize` first: an `argv` written as a string holding a list is read as the list, `overwrite: "true"` as true, and a `development_database_read` limit over 50, a history `window_days` over 90 or an `integration_test` timeout over 60 is clamped to that bound; each change is listed in the result's `adjusted`. `development_database_read` also takes `sort` ({field: 1|-1}); an unknown collection's refusal lists the real ones; `development_write` on an existing file without `overwrite` refuses as `DEVELOPMENT_FILE_EXISTS`. Each call is keyed by its native call id, so a replay after a crash returns the recorded result. Every text she writes in the turn is what she says, in order, text beside a tool call included; a message without a tool call ends the turn, and `stay_silent` ends it with nothing more to say: words she already wrote in the turn still leave, and a turn with none sends nothing; its result says which (`written`: how many texts the turn has written so far, counted by the Host from her session). Her instructions (`prompts/common.md`, and `prompts/turn.md` in her role system prompt) state the same contract, and every turn's notice ends with its first step in one line (`coordinator.THINK_FIRST`). The action brain uses the DSH loop and the tools granted to its task. `RETURNED` means the action turn returned; it does not establish that the requested goal was completed. The action brain returns a natural-language report; the program attaches the actual tool receipts. Delegation requires a workspace grant for the scene (the owner's local scene, or a channel route that names a workspace). The action brain's system prompt is `executor.md` plus who she is: with persona model `render.action_persona: core` (the default) her `core` sentences readable in the class of the conversation the task came from; with `values` only public persona sections tagged `render.values_tag`; with `persona` the persona document's every-turn sections readable in that class. It never receives memory, relationships, emotion or other state.

Where the program needs a model's answer before it can go on (the end of a character turn, the relevance gate, an appraisal, the action brain's report, a dialogue summary), the answer is checked deterministically: it stopped on its own, it has text (thinking alone is not an answer; tool calls count only for the action brain), and it has that point's shape. The end of a character turn needs a `think` first and final text that is not a JSON object and, outside her home conversations, not talk about tools or program blocks; its note asks her to write the whole unsent text again, since what she writes next replaces it; the gate needs 接话／不理 on its first line; an appraisal needs a JSON array. A failed check goes back to the model in the same session as a program note that names the problem, the way DSH returns a failed tool call (for her turn, in the same native turn), and the point asks again. After two repairs it fails with the named problem: her turn fails its episode, the gate lets the message pass quietly, an appraisal is skipped, an action ends `BLOCKED`, and a summary waits for its next run. Transport errors and empty completions are retried by DSH's own retry first, and an interrupted turn is not repaired. Each failed check is audited (`phase.rejected`, `execution.rejected`, `summary.rejected`).

Ordinary tool errors are returned to the active DSH action loop so it can inspect the error and continue. A task revision, cancellation, changed policy epoch, or expired lease fences further calls. The host records tool inputs and results as artifacts; result claims are checked against those records and any required effect receipts.

The active action lane uses DSH's native agent loop. Asuna does not add a fixed action-step termination limit or reject turns using a separate token-budget gate. Token measurements are observational; context pressure is handled through DSH compaction, using the selected native model metadata.

## Configuration

- `config/local.json` is the initial migration source. The example is `config/local.example.json`. After migration the DSH profile's Core `deployment`, `channelAdmission` and route settings are authoritative, and every secret is in DSH's credential store. Everything the profile writes goes to its data folder (the floor's `dataRoot`, default `$DSH_HOME/asuna/<profile>/`; the local chat works in `<data>/work/local-user`); locks that guard a database or an endpoint are per user (`ASUNA_LOCK_ROOT`, default `<temp>/asuna-locks`), so two profiles on one database still exclude each other.
- The two brains' models are DSH providers, defined with their keys on DSH's Models page. The Asuna settings card picks a provider and model for each brain from DSH's catalog; a brain without one uses DSH's default model (`agent-default-model`). The character and action routes are independent. Each route's reasoning effort is one of the levels DSH reports for its model, shown under DSH's names; the character route also sets the effort of her relevance gate (`attendEffort`) and of her group turns (`groupEffort`), empty meaning the route's own effort; the action route sets `idleMinutes`, how long a task's action context may sit unused before a continuation starts a fresh one (empty: 30), set from how long that route's provider keeps a cached prefix. The action brain may have a second route, `actionHigh` (the card's 高 row, with the same fields; empty provider: none, and no DSH default stands in for it). A level the model does not offer is refused (`INVALID_REASONING_EFFORT`). `config/local.json` has no model routes; one that still names `character` or `executor` is refused (`MODEL_ROUTES_ARE_DSH_PROVIDERS`).
- A model or embedding address is checked as DSH's Models page checks a provider: an http or https URL with a host.
- An adjacent `asuna-channel.local.json` is loaded only when `enabled` is `true`. Its example is `config/asuna-channel.example.json`.
- An adjacent `integration.local.json` provides the optional owner-bound managed integration profile. Its example is `config/integration.example.json`.
- Credentials, account IDs, scene IDs, provider addresses, and local paths come from deployment configuration. They are not fixed by lane name or stored in this contract.

Core's native plugin settings page uses DSH's settings mirror, staged form controls, revision fence and durable profile writes. A business secret is a reference in the settings, `{"$secret":"ASUNA_MONGO_URI"}`, named like an environment variable; its value lives only in DSH's credential store, which also holds the LLM provider keys. Any key whose name contains `mongo_uri`, `api_key`/`apikey`, `token`, `password` or `secret` must be such a reference. Core resolves the references when the worker starts or a proposal is validated and hands the values to the worker for that run; the settings page writes keys straight into the store and shows only whether each is configured. Save performs model-free validation. Apply quiesces ingress and background summaries at an idle user/action boundary, restarts the worker, and reloads an already-enabled adapter from the installed channel package. Each conversation already open is reopened at its next turn, once nothing of it is running, so it resumes with the applied routes (a live agent keeps the model it was opened with). A settings startup failure restores the previous runtime configuration while retaining the saved proposal for correction.

Native `asuna/stage` informational events attribute each actual request's `turn`, `step`, `operation`, `lane` and `phase`. Stage input notices also retain `source.lane`; `asuna/stage-result` links the final original assistant sequence to the same attribution. These envelopes are ignorable by plain DSH readers and contain no duplicate assistant content. Client projection uses DSH's location store and original assistant events, including native stream settlement and older-page loading. Brain names describe responsibilities, independently of provider/model. Only her turn's final text, accepted by the end-of-turn check and stored as `SPEAK` rows, is an outward expression; neither a successful model call nor a stored row is a platform delivery receipt. The worker ends each character turn with a `turn_done` event once it needs no further stage (a repair note continues the same turn).

Host restart marks unfinished managed actions `PAUSED` with `pause_reason=host_restart` and advances their fencing token; it does not enqueue action execution. A finished action whose result had not reached her is not paused: the handover watchdog hands it back. Native execution bindings, artifacts and results remain intact. A new explicit request in the existing local conversation may continue it with `task` op `message`, which reuses the prior execution binding under a new task grant. Internal opportunities and stale feedback cannot resume paused work. Pending feedback checks its original durable input's task/revision before and after model stages and at effect boundaries. The handover watchdog (`handover.py`) runs in the worker every minute, first one minute after start, and reads task state only. A finished task whose result is neither taken nor queued or being handed back is handed back again by its cause: a hand-back that failed before her turn gets the full result once more and then the short version (her brief, the report's first 3,000 characters with the length and page count of the rest, readable with `task` op `page`, and when the run finished, without tool records); one whose turn on it failed gets the short version; one lost without an error (a restart) gets the full result. When the short version fails too, the task's `feedback_state` becomes `UNDELIVERABLE` and the program leaves the developer a note in `developer_inbox` (`source.by: program`). A `RUNNING` task whose lease expired while the worker is not running it is closed as `BLOCKED` (`failure_type: ORPHANED`) and handed back. Elapsed time is never a failure; `claim` records `started_at`. Her task list (`task_state_from_program`) gives each task one `status` phrase instead of its raw state fields; a task carried on by a later one (`continues_task_id`) points to that one. `elsewhere_from_program` uses the same phrases. Cancellation, revision or restart pause suppresses further stages; completed public history is retained. An in-flight output may remain in diagnostics, but it cannot become another decision or publication after suppression.

`delegate {title, brief}` creates a task whose action session starts with her brief, followed by the program's facts (the original message, the workspace, attachments, grants). `task` op `message` on her own READY or RUNNING task records her words (`task_messages`) and injects them into the running action session at its next step; words that a run ended before reading start one more round in the same session. On a finished task it creates a continuation task on the same execution binding, unless the task's action context has been unused longer than the action route's `idleMinutes`: then the continuation gets its own binding and a fresh action session, opening with a line saying so, the previous run's report (at most 20,000 characters) and her new words (`fresh_context` on the task, decided once when she hands it over). A cancelled, paused (except by a new local request) or differently granted task refuses it in words. The results of `delegate` and of `task` op `message` always say, in `action_brain`, whether the action brain starts fresh or carries on, and, in `queue`, what is ahead of the task for the one action worker (how many, whether running and for how long, coarse places such as 家里 or 一个群, never a name), an empty queue included. A tool a task was not given is refused with one sentence from one table (`tasks.GIVEN_TO`, `capability_refusal`), by the plugin's guard and by the broker alike: who is given that tool, where this task came from, and that retrying will not change it. `task` op `stop` cancels a READY or RUNNING task and fences its execution. With an `actionHigh` route, `delegate` also takes `tier` (低 or 高, each described by what it suits, 低 when left out) and, for 高, `why` (at most 80 characters; missing, the call is refused with both descriptions); without it neither is listed. The task keeps `tier` {`chosen`, `why`, `ran`, `fell`}. A 高 task's action session opens on `actionHigh`'s options and each of its requests goes there (`tiers.js`) until a request ends in an error DSH's retries did not cure (any code but `ABORTED` and `CONTEXT_WINDOW_EXCEEDED`): that request is retried on the action route, and the task stays there (`fell`: reason `refused`, `down`, `busy` or `owner`, and `mid_run` when the session already had an answer from 高). A failure of the line (`SERVER`, `TIMEOUT`, `TRANSPORT`, `STREAM_CLOSED`, `RATE_LIMIT`) makes 高 rest for `actionHigh`'s `idleMinutes`; a key, credential or quota failure, with no clock, until the settings are applied again or the Host restarts; a refusal does not rest it. A 高 task begun while 高 rests runs on the action route from the start. A continuation in the same context keeps the tier it ran at; a fresh one starts again at the tier she chose, and the idle limit is that tier's route's. She reads all of this in words that name no model, provider, price or quota (`action_tiers.REASONS`): `delegate`'s `tier` (`交给高档`, or that 高 is resting with `最早 HH:MM 再试` or `要等主人处理，没有钟点`), a task's `tier` in `task_state_from_program` (the tier, or `选了高，跑的是低：` and why, marked when it fell mid-run), and the weekly tooling review's `action_brain.tiers` (chosen and ran per tier, falls by reason, and each `why` beside the steps run and how the task ended). The settings card shows 高's rest on its row (`actionHigh` in the status). `tier.fell` and `tier.rest` are audited. Her words never add a grant: a task keeps the grants of the turn that created it. Tool calls carry their immutable operation binding; a delayed old call cannot acquire the successor's grant from a mutable session record. Task context prioritizes live work and then durable input time, rather than the number of database writes, so recently returned work remains available for continuation.

The action brain's report reaches her in pages of at most 6,000 characters (`tasks.report_pages`; a page ends before the last section that starts in its second half — a Markdown heading, an all-bold line, 【…】 or 一、/1. numbering — else at a blank line or a line break in its last fifth): the hand-back carries the first page, which says which page it is, how long the report is and how to turn to the next (`report_pages` in the result); the result she is shown counts the run's tool records (`tool_records`) and leaves out their ids and the stored `facts` (the report again with every record id), and her task list marks a recent task whose report is longer (`report`). `task {op: page, task, page}`, with the action tools, reads that page of a task's stored report, unchanged, without running the action brain: at home any of her tasks, elsewhere only a task this conversation sent.

Outside the owner's own conversations, what she reads of a report holds nothing of the machine (`machine_words.py`): in the hand-back (its report, tool-record excerpts and uncertainties), in `task` `page` and in the action brain's answers that `ask` `read` shows, a Python traceback reads 「（程序出错：<type>）」, an absolute path under the checkout or the data folder reads as its path from there, a path in the action brain's own folder (`/task/...`) stays, and any other absolute path (judged by shape: a drive letter, a share, a POSIX path of two or more parts) reads 「〔本机路径〕」. It is a rewrite, never a refusal; the stored result and every home conversation keep the original.

Continued tasks on the same authorized execution binding reuse their actual native action session via `agents.create/resume`. Each task receives its current grant and system context; a different role, actor or policy epoch cannot import the old source. The plugin admits a successor after the preceding native Turn and handle teardown, without blocking its business result acknowledgement. Her conversation shows each task as one collaboration thread, recorded as `asuna/collab` events in her session: her brief and later messages, the action brain's questions, progress notes and reports, her answers, status changes, where a finished run's result stands on its way back to her (handing, taken once her turn on it finishes, or missed: never handed back, her turn on it failed, or a Host restart came first; then handed back again, or final), and the action brain's own work between them as ranges of its native session (never copies of its records). Entries carry ids, so a replay adds nothing; entries that arrive during one of her turns are appended once she is idle. Receipt replay does not execute a completed stage. The business-bound action preset uses foreground native handles; it does not accept generic human prompts through DSH's continuation manager, which inherits the role preset rather than the required action composition.

## Channel API

When channel routes are configured, the host binds the channel server to `127.0.0.1`; the default port is `8766`. It is separate from the Web UI and is not exposed through the Web proxy. Each configured channel has a unique bearer token of at least 24 characters. A route binds the platform account, sender, Asuna person, scene, and publication target.

A configured channel's id names its platform (`channels.qq`), and that platform's channel package must be installed. A channel package (`packages/channels/napcat-qq`) calls `registerChannel({kind, title, project, resource_root, python, module, integration_directory, skill_directories})`. Its Python kind module gives the platform's id formats (`qq:<account>`, `qq:<bot>:<dm|group>:<target>`), how the adapter writes a real @ in inbound text and how her @ reaches it, the media hosts vision may fetch from by default, and the adapter's derived settings. Its adapter is the integration that her owner-granted `integration_*` tools develop and run, from the package's own development project. Its skill joins her skill directories. A platform's conversations live in a workspace named by its title.

`channelAdmission=automatic` admits authenticated, valid platform messages from previously unknown DMs/groups/members. The adapter derives `auto-dm-<id>` / `auto-group-<id>` route IDs; existing configured targets keep their routes. Core persists admissions in the existing `artifacts` collection and creates separate per-member channel workspaces without owner grants. An automatically admitted group member does not start another conversation or bump the group's authorization epoch. Removals/blocking revoke membership; old task epochs remain fenced. `explicit` requires configured enrollment. Both modes honor channel `blocked_senders` and `blocked_groups` before admission and outbox delivery.

Receipt projects to the target's real native QQ conversation before inference, including non-waking group activity. It uses the original durable input ID to deduplicate; an acknowledgment in `sink_receipts` permits projection retry after interruption without replaying execution. DSH requires a conversation's first visible entry to be the system head her agent writes on its first step, so until she has had a turn in a conversation its lines wait in that conversation's DSH inbox (next-step, the newest 40; older ones stay in Memory). Her first step there claims them right after the head, in arrival order and before the stage notice, which leaves out of its history what they already show. A line that arrives while that first turn is starting waits for its head. Later a line joins the conversation as it arrives, except while one of her tool calls waits for its result: then it joins at the next step boundary, after the result, as DSH places its own input, so a call and its result stay adjacent (some providers refuse anything between them). While a compaction runs on the conversation (its `compaction/start` without a `compaction/end`), a line waits for the compaction's end, committed or failed, and then joins: a compaction in her turn refuses a summary of a surface that changed under it. A waiting line is held in the Host's memory only, and its receipt is acknowledged (`sink_receipts`) once it has joined the conversation and the conversation is flushed; a stop before that leaves it unacknowledged, and the next start projects it again. A line written between them before this rule is emptied in place (a DSH surface replace) when the conversation is opened. DSH lists a conversation in the sidebar from its first turn. The Web composer is view-only for QQ and every platform conversation except a line the owner joins (`OWNER_JOINS`), where it posts as the owner with his grants; native archive hides until the next platform activity.

All endpoints require:

```http
Authorization: Bearer <channel-token>
```

POST bodies are JSON objects, limited to 256 KiB. Unsupported envelope fields are rejected.

A channel kind that declares `ROUTE_KEYS` (`agent`) also takes a route key: `route-<route>.<hex>`, the HMAC-SHA256 of
`asuna-route-key:<route>` under the channel's token (`channels.route_key`). A route key posts events only as its own
route, claims only its own line's words (`/outbox` filtered by the route's target; group admin actions are never
handed out), and confirms only those (`/receipt`); every other endpoint, another route and another channel answer 403.
While she keeps that line closed, its claim returns `{"items":[],"line":"closed"}` and her words wait. On a kind that
declares `OWNER_JOINS` (`agent`), the local owner is a member of every line and writes there in the Web composer as
the owner; a route key's claim also returns, once each and only while the line is open, what the owner wrote there
(`"owner_lines":[{"id","seq","text","at"}]`, oldest first; `seq` is its place in the conversation, at most 20 a claim), and her answer to him is claimed like any other. At start the
host writes each such route's key to `<data>/private/route-keys/<channel>-<route>.json` (`url`, `route_id`,
`account_id`, `sender_id`, `key`) and removes keys of routes no longer configured. A route's optional `display_name`
(1–40 characters) is set on its person's identity at start.

### Receive an event

An inbound identity binds the channel, account, scene and platform event ID. When `occurred_at` is supplied, its value is part of that identity too: the same ID and send time is a redelivery, while the same ID at a different time is a new message. Adapters supply the original platform send time, or omit the field when unavailable; they do not substitute the time of forwarding. A reply resolves within its own scene and policy epoch to the newest matching inbound occurrence or delivered outbound receipt no later than the replying message.

```http
POST /v1/channels/{channel_id}/events
Content-Type: application/json
```

Direct message example:

```json
{
  "route_id": "authorized-dm",
  "account_id": "configured-account",
  "sender_id": "configured-sender",
  "event_id": "platform-event-id",
  "text": "Message text",
  "occurred_at": "2026-01-01T00:00:00Z"
}
```

`route_id`, `account_id`, `sender_id`, `event_id`, and `text` are required. `occurred_at` and `raw` are optional. Text is limited to 16,000 characters. The host derives person, scene, and target from the route; callers cannot supply a scope, task, or internal episode kind. Of `raw`, the host stores only the sender profile in `raw.asuna_peer` after checking it against the authenticated sender and group, the profiles of the people a group message @-mentions in `raw.asuna_mentioned` (each bound to an account in `mentioned_account_ids` and this group; they name those people in the group, as a speaker's profile does), the media block in `raw.asuna_media`, `raw.group_name`, and `raw.asuna_catchup` (`reason` `startup` or `reconnect`, `fetched_at`) on a line the adapter fetched from the platform's history after a gap, and `raw.asuna_notice` on a platform notice (below); the platform event itself is not stored. A caught-up line was said at its `occurred_at`: the line she reads carries that time with 补读, and the history windows and the relevance gate count it from then (`caught_up.said_at`). Every QQ person, configured or automatically admitted, is `qq:<account>`.

Group events use the same endpoint and add `group_id`, `mentioned_account_ids`, and optionally `reply_to`. A direct-message event may carry `reply_to` too (the line it quotes); the host binds it to the line it quotes in that chat (`event.reply`: `reply_to`, `reply_message_id`). These values must come from the adapter's parsed platform event. Direct-message routes reject `group_id` and `mentioned_account_ids`. A message that only quotes another, with no words of its own, carries `raw.asuna_reply_only: true`; the host words it. The host checks the configured group and member bindings. A group event wakes the character only under the configured mention and saved-reply rules; other authorized group messages can be persisted without starting a character turn.

Quoting is hers to choose, in a group or a private chat: `speak`'s `quote` names the line by its number there (as shown, brackets or not) or `这条` (the line that called her); left out, nothing is quoted, and the program never chooses a quote. The quote rides on her first text or picture part of that call (a sticker, poke or reaction cannot carry one); the result echoes it (`quoted`: `引的是 〔n〕 <speaker>：<first words>`). A line she can point at is anyone's line in that conversation or hers once delivered, never a program notice (`People.line_of`); a number that is not one, or anything else, is refused with the conversation's last six lines by number, speaker and first words. Only in a turn where others have spoken since the line that called her, `speak_from_program.quote` lists their numbers (`叫你那条之后有 N 条新话：〔n〕、…`, at most 8). `publish._quote` turns her choice into the platform id (`platform_reply_to`), and the line her words answer is kept apart (`platform_answers`).

Which lines of a group reach her depends on what the line is and on the group's state (`focus.py`):

| What the line is | Active group | Resting group |
|---|---|---|
| Addressed to her: an @ of her account, a reply to a line of hers | her full turn, no gate | her full turn, no gate |
| A gesture at her: a poke, a reaction to a line of hers (a platform notice) | the relevance gate | the relevance gate |
| Maybe for her: an awaited answer, an answer while her question waits, someone on her watchlist, a caught-up @ or reply | the relevance gate | the relevance gate |
| Anything else | the relevance gate at a few moments (a thread that called her, her name without an @, the group's proactive rules, a chain); otherwise nothing | nothing |

A group message wakes her for one of these reasons. An @ of her account or a reply to a message of hers runs her full turn, unless it is a caught-up line older than 5 minutes (`caught_up.STALE_SECONDS`): then it is `wake_reason: catchup_mention`, through the gate, and a full turn carries `group_continuity_from_program.caught_up_from_program`. The others go through the relevance gate first:

- a reply inside a thread that woke her in the last 30 minutes (the quoted line had a wake reason; a line of hers need not be in it);
- her name without an @ (her display name or the persona's `people.self_names`);
- the proactive rules of a group that opted in;
- a chain (below);
- an awaited answer: her newest delivered line in the group was addressed to someone — the people she @-tagged by label and the author of the line she answered (`channels.addressees`), waited on by default like a reply — and within `channels.AWAIT_SECONDS` (180 s; in her groups 90% of human answers come within about 3 minutes) one of them writes a line that @s and quotes no one. `await_answer` (`wait`: `no` turns it off for the turn, as does `speak`'s `wait` `no` or `question`, a question being waited on by its own looks and answers; `yes` with `why` waits for anyone when the line is addressed to nobody). One look per line of hers (`wake_reason: awaited_answer`); her turn carries `awaited_from_program`. Apart from that look, `waiting_from_program` lists who she still waits for (`waiting.py`): her delivered lines of the last hour in a group that @ someone by label or carry `await_answer` `why` (then anyone there), not turned off, whose people have not written in that group since; each entry gives who, how long ago in words and the first 30 characters of her line. A group turn shows that group's; a heartbeat or a plan due at home shows every group's, at most 5. It is computed from the stored lines each time; a plain reply does not count.
- her question waits: within ten minutes of a question of hers in that group (`speak` `wait=question`), a person who writes a line that @s and quotes no one gets her one look (`wake_reason: question_wait`), once each; resting groups let it through;
- a platform notice that asks for a look (`raw.asuna_notice.gate`): `poked` (someone poked her) or `reacted` (someone reacted to a line of hers); both also in a group she lets rest, where her relevance gate decides;
- someone on her watchlist (`watches.py`): the `watch` tool (`do` `on` with `person` — a label, `#number`, name or id that names exactly one person in the conversation she is in — `hours` 1–72, `how_often` `once` (default: the first time they speak, then done) or `burst` (again after 10 quiet minutes, until it expires) and an optional `why`; `do` `off` with the `id`) keeps at most 5 people in `watches`, one per person. A line from a watched person in any of her conversations sets `event.watched`; in a group whose line has no other wake reason it is `wake_reason: watched`, one look through the gate. In a private chat the line is already her turn. Her turn carries `watched_from_program`, and every turn `watching_from_program` (who, roughly how long is left, the mode, and the why only in a turn of the class she wrote it in). An expired watch is not a turn: the next turn's block carries one `ran_out` line on how often they appeared, once. The tool is offered in groups and private chats, and anywhere while the list is not empty. Nothing a watched person says is copied to another conversation.

The gate is one short request in a small child session of its own for each question (`asuna-attend-…`, preset `asuna-attend`), its repairs included; it keeps no history and never compacts. It runs on the character route at a low effort. Its system prompt is the shared rules and her `core` sentences (public) as who she is, not her persona's rules for talking. It reads only words:
- why she is asked;
- the speaker's label, notes and familiarity;
- her notes for this group (its always-shown sections in order, the first `attend.NOTES_CHARS` = 800 characters) and what she wrote about the speaker (the person layer, then this group's, the first `attend.UNDERSTANDING_CHARS` = 400), cut the same way every time; nothing from her other conversations;
- the lines since she last spoke there (at least 10, at most 60 minutes back, at most 40 lines);
- how long ago that was;
- her public mood.

She answers 接话 or 不理 with a short reason. An answer in another shape is told so and asked again; after two repairs, or when the gate cannot answer at all, the message passes quietly as `ATTEND_FAILED` (`attend.choice: failed`, `silent_reason` 没判出来…), never counted as her 不理, and notices it carried stay for her next look; her home turns show how many looks failed in the last day (`host_from_program.missed_looks`). 不理 commits the episode before any recall (`processing_outcome: ATTEND_QUIET`). 接话 prepares her full context, says her reason in `attend_from_program`, and continues (`ATTEND_JOIN`). Every other message is stored without a turn.

A delivered line of hers whose platform read-back stored fewer parts than were sent (`segment_mismatch`: a QQ face, a quote, an @) carries `not_landed`, naming the missing part in words; the line stays delivered. In a group, a turn's history covers the same catch-up window, and never fewer than the last 12 lines. Every line says when it was said on her clock face: a platform line entering her conversation ends its label line with the local month/day and time (`[名字 #4] 10/5 08:19`), and a history row carries `at` in the same form instead of raw timestamps, plus `arrived` when it reached the other side in another minute (a caught-up line, a late delivery). A group turn also carries `pace_from_program` (versioned word tables in `places.py`, nothing advisory): when the last line was (its clock stamp), when the current stretch of talk began (a pause of 30 minutes or more ends a stretch) and how much it holds, and the pause before it with how much has been said since the line before that pause. Whether an older line is still the topic is hers to judge. `deployment.reasoning_effort` sets the character route's effort per stage, where the model offers it: `attend` (default `low`) for the gate, and `group` for group turns. The local chat keeps the route's own effort.

The host persists accepted input before any retrieval or model call and returns:

```json
{"status":"accepted","episode_id":"ep-…","received_at":"…"}
```

An identical event for the same channel, account, route scene, and platform event ID returns `duplicate` without a second queue entry. On a peer line she has closed herself (a direct-message route of a channel kind that declares `PEER_LINE`), the host returns `{"status":"line_closed"}` and keeps nothing: the message is passed over, not queued for later. Reusing an event ID with conflicting identity or content is rejected. `accepted` confirms durable host receipt, not a reply or publication.

Adapters that normalize media may provide bounded metadata under `raw.asuna_media`. The host does not interpret arbitrary platform payloads. Media metadata and an image placeholder do not mean the character brain has seen the image; image reading is described below.

#### Platform notices

A notice (a recall, a poke, a reaction, …) comes as an event from the person who did it, its `text` the adapter's words for it, with `raw.asuna_notice`: `kind` (the adapter's name, for the audit), optional `gate` (`poked`, `reacted`) and optional `line` (`message_id` of the line it is about, and `mine` when that line is hers). It passes the same checks as a message: route, member, account; a blocked person's notice is dropped (`{"status": "dropped"}`), and nothing a watched person does that way wakes her. `{line}` in the text is filled by the host (`notices.py`) as every pointer reads (below: the line's number where the conversation shows numbers, its speaker, hers as `你`, and its first 40 characters): a line of hers however old, anyone's among this conversation's lines of the last day, else `一条消息`. She reads it named when she reads it, from the notice's own words, so the label in it is the program's. A reaction asks for a look only when it is on her line; one between other people is kept like a notice without a gate. A name, @ or label inside the words reads as in a message. She reads a notice as the program's line, not as the person's words: its label line ends with `（平台通知）`, and a stored one carries `notice` in her history rows.

- In a group, a notice with a `gate` goes through the relevance gate like a line that calls her, and is stored as that look's input.
- In a group, a notice without one (or one that does not wake her in a group she lets rest) is kept in `artifacts` (kind `held_notice`, at most 50 per conversation), not as a line of the conversation: `{"status": "held"}`. The next turn in that conversation carries it once in `notices_from_program` (`items`: one line each, `[label] time（平台通知）words`, lines of one kind with the same words folded into one with every label; reactions between other people folded into one line `这期间的点表情：…（平台通知）`, the 5 newest lines reacted to and the rest counted (`另有 N 条较早的`); at most 10 after folding, older ones counted in `more`), and so does the relevance gate's material, after the lines; a quiet answer there ends them, a taken-up one leaves them for the turn. Shown, they are deleted; a Host restart keeps what was not shown yet.
- In a private chat a notice is that chat's input and wakes her as a message does.

### Post a notice for her home

```http
POST /v1/channels/{channel_id}/notices
Content-Type: application/json
```

```json
{"kind": "request_friend", "words": "想加你好友，附言「你好」", "actor_name": "name",
 "request": {"flag": "platform-flag", "type": "friend"}}
```

A notice about her own account or one that asks her to act: a friend or group request, a kick, a change of her admin role. `words` (up to 200 characters) follow `actor_name` (shown in 「」 with label characters removed); `{g}` in them becomes the group's name, from her conversation there or `group_name`; `group_id` and `at` (platform epoch seconds) are optional. `request` (`flag`, `type` `friend` or `group`, and `sub_type` `add` or `invite` for a group) makes it a request she can answer: it is kept in `artifacts` (kind `platform_request`, one row per flag, so the same request sent again stays one) with a number (`请求1`, …) given once and never reused, open until she answers it. Her answer's receipt marks it `answered`, or `gone` when the platform refused the answer (the request is no longer there), or `unknown`. At each Host start, the requests still open are listed once in a program event in her local chat, each with how to answer it. Notices are held for a minute, then one program event in her local chat lists them, each with its time on her clock and, for a request, how to answer it: `group_action` with `kind` `accept` or `decline` and `who` the number (`GROUP_ACTION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` otherwise, `GROUP_ACTION_ANSWER_AT_HOME` elsewhere). Her local chat lists `group_action` when a configured channel's kind declares `REQUESTS`; a turn may use it while a request waits. The answer goes out like an admin action, with target `{"type": "request", "id": "friend" | "group"}` and `admin` carrying the request's `flag`. Accepting admits nobody: who may talk to her stays the channel's admission setting. The answer is `{"status": "held"}`; refusals `403 NOTICE_FIELD_DENIED`, `400 NOTICE_INVALID`.

### Claim public output

```http
GET /v1/channels/{channel_id}/outbox?wait_seconds=25&supports=image
```

`wait_seconds` is capped at 25. The host atomically claims a queued public `SPEAK` message and returns its frozen target, text, publication ID, attempt ID, `reply_to` (the platform id of the line her words answer, for a platform that needs a reply token; never shown as a quote) and `quote` (the platform id of the line she chose to quote, or none; only an adapter that declares `quote` shows it). Internal monologue, reasoning, traces, and tool output are never placed in the outbox.

`supports` is an optional comma-separated list of what the adapter can handle today (`supports=image,sticker`). An adapter that does not declare a capability receives text only. The host keeps each channel's last declaration (`capabilities.py`, `artifacts` kind `channel_capabilities`), across restarts, and logs once per run the pointer capabilities a channel does not declare: `quote` (her line can quote another), `quote_in` (it passes on what others quote), `react` (she can react), `react_in` (it passes on reactions), `recall` (it carries recalls). What a channel does not declare is not carried. When a message carries an accepted image and the adapter declared `image`, the item also carries `attachment` metadata — `{artifact_id, media_type, sha256, size}`, never base64; the bytes come from the endpoint below. When the adapter did not declare it, the text still goes out and the message row records that the picture did not (`attachment_skipped`), so delivered history cannot later claim an image arrived that never left the host. A message that is one sticker is offered only to an adapter that declared `sticker`: its item carries `text: ""` and `sticker` — `{"kind": "custom"}` with the picture as `attachment` (send it as a picture marked as a sticker), or `{"kind": "market", "emoji_id", "emoji_package_id", "key", "summary"}` (send the store sticker by its ids, no bytes). Without `sticker` declared the row is failed with `CHANNEL_DOES_NOT_DECLARE_STICKER`. Her words may carry `[表情:名字]`, a platform face by name, for the adapter to turn into the real face.

The adapter sends only the returned text — plus the declared attachment, when the item carries one — to the returned target. Starting an adapter or claiming a message is not a platform receipt.

Where the conversation's channel kind declares `REPLY_PARTS` (`channel_kinds.reply_parts`; the NapCat QQ kind: text, sticker, picture, poke, react, recall; the QQ Bot kind: text, picture), she speaks only through `speak` (the old name `reply` runs as it, unlisted, with a note), which is listed there in place of `await_answer` and `attach_image`. A role session's tool definitions are given when the session starts (`role_tool_specs` with the session id, `role_tools.session_specs`) and stay fixed for it: `speak` lists a react or recall part only where the channel's adapter declared `react` / `recall`, and `quote` only where it declared `quote`; its description and part fields name only what is listed. The audit row `session.tools` keeps the definitions' hash. A part or a quote the channel does not declare is refused in her words (`这条腿做不了点表情`, `这条腿做不了撤回自己的话`, `quote：这条腿做不了引用`). When a channel's declaration loses or regains `quote`, `react` or `recall`, each of its conversations reads it once, at its next turn (`speak_from_program.leg`: `这条腿做不了引用。`, `这条腿又能引用了。`; `artifacts` kind `capability_change`); a channel's first declaration tells nothing. Its `parts` go out in order, each its own outbox item (`reply_part`) queued and published during the call, with `not_before` by typing pace (`speak.chars_per_second`, clamped to `speak.min_gap_s`…`speak.max_gap_s`); a part is claimable only after the one before it settled, and a failed or unknown part cancels the rest (`CANCELLED_AFTER_FAILURE`). Every part is checked before any is queued: one wrong part queues nothing, and the result names each wrong part and why. Text parts hold at most `speak.max_messages` lines in a turn, across its calls (`role_tools.chat_lines`: every line counts, a blank one does not, a fenced code block counts as one); the same text twice in one turn is refused, as is a sticker token or `[分条]` inside a text, and a `#n` outside a label that is the number of someone in that conversation, alone or after `@name` (`People.numbers`; the result says whose number it is; `force=yes` sends the text as written). A sticker part names a sticker she keeps; a picture part names an artifact in `image_artifacts_from_program` and may leave without words; a poke part names a person by label (in a private chat, left out: the other person); a react part names a line as a quote does (`line`: its number or `这条`; not one of hers) and a face by name. A recall part takes back a line of hers (`line`: its number; `why` required, at most 100 characters, refused if longer): still queued, it is cancelled and never leaves (`CANCELLED_BY_HER`, the row keeps why; later parts do not wait on it); delivered within the platform's window (the channel kind's `OWN_RECALL_SECONDS`, 120 for QQ; none in a group where she is owner or admin), it is an action row the adapter does (`recall`, declared on the claim), whose history line reads `撤回 〔n〕 你：…（为什么：…）`; later, it is refused with what she can do instead. The result says what happened (`recall`: `撤回 〔n〕 交给平台了（还没确认）` or `取消了 〔n〕，那句没发出去`). A done recall marks her line (`recalled_at`; history rows show `recalled: 你撤回了这句`); one the platform refused or left unanswered reads `撤回没成：平台没执行（…）` in her undelivered lines. Someone else's line is not hers to take back (`group_action` recall, as an admin). A poke or reaction is a row in her history in the program's words, marked `（你做的动作，不是你说的话）`; it is handed only to an adapter whose claim declares `poke` / `react` (`CHANNEL_DOES_NOT_DECLARE_POKE` otherwise), and its receipt carries no message id. A reaction a private chat refuses is not tried there again until the Host restarts, and a session that starts meanwhile is not shown the react part in that channel's private chats. `quote` rides on the call (above), and in a group `wait` does (`no`: no awaited answer; `question`: a question to the people there, with `patience`, see "Her questions"). Body text in such a conversation never leaves: a turn that ends with it and without `speak`, `stay_silent` or not, is handed back once with the words whole as written (`这段没发出去：「…」。在这里说话用 speak。`), and words left unsent are told once in her next turn there (`unsent_from_program`); words she also sent through `speak` in that turn are not unsent. `stay_silent`'s `why` may be left out (after speaking there is nothing to explain); the record then reads （没写为什么）. There `stay_silent`'s result says how many parts `speak` queued this turn (`sent`), and `speak_from_program.sent` says that only `speak` speaks and how many lines a turn may hold. Parts a turn queued still leave when the Host stops mid-turn: recovery publishes them before the turn resumes (`Coordinator.publish_queued`).

Elsewhere, what she says is every text she writes in the turn, in order — text written beside a tool call included, since her conversation shows each as hers — as one message, its texts as paragraphs. A received line joins her conversation as it arrives, so a running turn sees lines that arrive between its steps. A line belongs to the first turn that had it in view (it reached that turn's model request): when that turn ends with speech or a deliberate silence, every such line still queued for its own turn is marked `absorbed_by` that episode and completes as `SEEN_IN_TURN` without a model call, so one line is never answered twice. A line that arrives after the turn's last request is not in view and gets its own turn. Platform turns are told both rules (`speak_from_program`).

### Fetch attachment bytes

```http
GET /v1/channels/{channel_id}/outbox/{publication_id}/attachment?attempt_id=…&artifact_id=…
Authorization: Bearer <channel token>
```

The same channel token authorizes this request. It returns the bytes of the one artifact that this publication's own claim declared, with `Content-Type` taken from the file signature. Only a publication currently in `SENDING` whose `attempt_id` matches the request may read it. The artifact must belong to that message's own scope, and the stored bytes are re-verified against the row's `sha256` before anything is written to the socket. `artifact_id` is optional and only ever narrows the request: a different artifact is refused, never substituted.

A row that records `attachment_skipped` is treated as never having declared the picture: its metadata stays on the row so delivered history can say the message was meant to carry one, and the byte endpoint refuses it with `ATTACHMENT_NOT_DECLARED` rather than handing out bytes for an image that never went out. A later claim that does carry the image clears that marker, so the flag always describes the current attempt.

Refusals are `403` with a code — `PUBLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `PUBLICATION_ATTEMPT_MISMATCH`, `ATTACHMENT_NOT_DECLARED`, `ATTACHMENT_ARTIFACT_DENIED`, `ATTACHMENT_SCOPE_DENIED`, `ATTACHMENT_SHA_MISMATCH`, `ATTACHMENT_HASH_MISMATCH`, `ATTACHMENT_NOT_AN_IMAGE`, `ATTACHMENT_OVER_LIMIT`, `ATTACHMENT_MEDIA_TYPE_MISMATCH` — and carry no partial body. The adapter re-checks the digest and the file signature on its side. Neither side treats a successful claim as proof the image arrived: only a platform receipt says that.

### Post her own mute in a group

```http
POST /v1/channels/{channel_id}/own_mute
Content-Type: application/json
```

```json
{"group_id": "group-id", "source": "notice", "muted": true, "whole_group": false, "operator_id": "10001"}
```

Whether her own account is muted in one admitted group (not a blocked one). `source` is `notice` (the platform said she, or the whole group, was just muted or unmuted) or `lookup` (the adapter read her state from the platform; a lookup that finds no mute clears any). The row also ends by itself when `until` passes. A send is always attempted, muted or not.

While she is muted in a group, every turn there (a call, a reply, a gate-passed line, a visit) carries `muted_here_from_program` right before `event`, sent in every notice like the trigger: one line saying she is muted (or that the whole group is), until when on her clock or that it is not known, and by whom by their label there or that it is not known, and that her words do not go out. Her line refused this way reads `没发出去：你在这个群被禁言了` in `undelivered_outbound_not_public`. At home, her places (`places_from_program`) mark such a group with `muted_you`, the same words for choosing whether to go; the visit itself stays allowed.

### Record a platform receipt

```http
POST /v1/channels/{channel_id}/outbox/{publication_id}/receipt
Content-Type: application/json
```

```json
{
  "attempt_id": "claimed-attempt-id",
  "status": "platform_accepted",
  "platform_message_id": "platform-message-id",
  "response": {"platform": "response"}
}
```

`status` is `platform_accepted`, `failed`, or `unknown`; `response` must be an object. `platform_accepted` requires a non-empty `platform_message_id` and records `DELIVERED` with `delivery_basis=platform_ack`. It means the platform accepted the message, not that a person read it. A matching receipt may be repeated idempotently; a conflicting attempt or terminal receipt is rejected.

If the send result cannot be determined, the adapter records `unknown`. A host restart converts an in-flight `SENDING` attempt to `UNKNOWN`. The host does not automatically claim it again. A later definitive receipt for that same attempt may resolve the state.

### HTTP outcomes

- `400` — malformed JSON, invalid size, or invalid field values.
- `403` — authentication, route, identity, capability, or receipt denied.
- `404` — unknown channel endpoint.
- `503` — host-side service failure; the host records a redacted diagnostic.

## Action capabilities

Action tool visibility is bound to the persisted task grant and route configuration. The action lane receives the DSH-native `skill`, `todo_write`, `web_search`, and `web_fetch` capabilities, plus Asuna tools allowed for that task. `web_search` is DSH's tool rebuilt from its exported parts (`registerWebSearch`) with 10 sources and a `detail` argument (`titles`: title, link and date only); snippets are capped at `search.snippet_chars` (300) and publication times are put on the conversation's clock from the session binding's `timezone`. Its backend is the cognition core's `asuna-search` provider on DSH's web service (`packages/cognition-core/src/search.js`), pinned by the installer, which tries `deployment.search.order` (default `searxng`, `gemini`, `exa`, `deepseek-official`) per query: SearXNG at most once per cooldown and resting after a failure or an all-blocked empty answer, Gemini with Grounding with Google Search (its cited pages as sources, redirect links resolved; resting after a 429 until the named quota refreshes), Exa through DSH's provider with a credential reference, other ids through providers registered on the service. A resting, unconfigured, failing or empty backend passes the query on; an empty answer is returned only when none had sources. Each search appends a log-only `asuna/web-search` event to the calling session. Settings and behaviour: [RUN_ASUNA.md](RUN_ASUNA.md#web-search). `web_fetch` is DSH's tool over the core's `asuna-fetch` provider (`fetch.js`): DSH's HTTP provider with a 5 MB body limit, HTML reduced to title and body without scripts, styles, SVG or templates, and a Cloudflare challenge (403/503 loading `/cdn-cgi/challenge-platform/`) returned as `WEB_HUMAN_VERIFICATION` ([RUN_ASUNA.md](RUN_ASUNA.md#web-fetch)). Skills (`skills.skill_directories`): a job from the owner's private conversations sees every skill root; a job from a group sees only the skills whose `SKILL.md` header lists `group` in `scene_kinds` (`home`, `group`, `dm`; absent means `home`), and one from someone else's chat those listing `dm`, each passed as a root of its own; publication refuses a header with an unknown key or kind (`python -m asuna.skills`).

Commands the worker does not write itself run under DSH's own sandbox: the Host wraps each one with `ctx.sandbox.confine` (dsh-sandbox-local — a restricted token on Windows, bubblewrap or Landlock on Linux, Seatbelt on macOS) in `workspace-write` mode, so it may write only its own folder: a task's folder for `sandbox_run`, the candidate for `development_run`, the output folder for a persona job, the data folder for an integration run. Reads and the network are not restricted. Which sessions may run commands at all is decided by the tools a task is given: `sandbox_run` is offered only to tasks from her owner-private conversations (her local chat, the owner's DM, and her home lines: the peer line and the agent lines), and elsewhere her capability list says the action brain cannot run code there. Commands are native to the machine: `python3` is the worker's Python, and an argument `/task` names the task folder. A command runs 30 seconds (`development_run` 35) unless its call gives `timeout` (seconds, at most 600); one stopped by its time returns its output so far with `timed_out` and `stopped`. Output is capped at 256 KiB. A command's `TEMP`/`TMP`/`TMPDIR` is `.tmp` inside its own folder (for `development_run`, inside a core candidate), and its Pythons load `asuna/sandbox_site` so that `tempfile` folders keep the command's write grant on Windows. A command runs as the Host's own user: `ssh` uses that user's keys, `git` in a `sandbox_run` command logs in to HTTPS remotes with that user's logins, nothing prompts (`GIT_TERMINAL_PROMPT=0`, `GCM_INTERACTIVE=never`), and when the local config sets `character_git` {`name`, `email`} what her commands commit carries that name as author and committer. On Windows the sandbox's restricted token has no SSPI credentials, so Schannel cannot open HTTPS there (`curl.exe` fails; Python's own HTTPS works) and git uses its bundled OpenSSL and CA file (`http.sslBackend=openssl`, set through `GIT_CONFIG_COUNT`). MSYS programs such as Git for Windows' `sh.exe` do not start there, so neither do git's credential helpers (the list is emptied), hooks or `!` aliases; a `sandbox_run` command's git gets the login from `asuna/sandbox_site/git-askpass.cmd` (`GIT_ASKPASS`), which asks Git Credential Manager and hands git only the part it asked for. Each home place's working folder (the local chat's, and each home line's: every agent line and the peer line; `grants.home_workspaces`) holds her own working copy, `working-copy`, so `/task/working-copy` is one in every home place; platform places have none. Each is a separate full clone of the local checkout (the Host's `repository` development project) whose `origin` is the checkout's origin. The Host makes each at start when it is missing and never changes one that is there; the checkout itself stays outside what her commands may write, so a command cannot fetch into it and its `origin/*` moves only when the Host fetches. A home task's commands also get `ASUNA_MONGO_URI`, `ASUNA_DATABASE` and `ASUNA_TIMEZONE` (her zone) for `asuna.dbread`: find, count, distinct, aggregate (no `$out`/`$merge`) and a conversation's history with its archive, each answer repeating its query (with a find's sort and skip) with the rows found (零行 when none), `local_window` turning her local times into UTC bounds (`now` and `recent(hours)` for her clock now and the last hours), `place` naming the conversation a place code stands for; core skill `asuna-database` explains it. `list_files`, `read_file` and `write_file` are done by the worker itself inside the task folder, protected sources read-only. The Host says at initialize whether it can confine; `deployment.sandbox.backend` is `auto` (default) or `none`. Without a Host sandbox, or with `none`, `sandbox_run`, the managed integration process, persona jobs and self-development are off: the tools are not offered, her capability list says these cannot be handed over, and the settings card's status line names the backend (`dsh`) or the reason there is none. On Windows the Host must be able to change permissions on the data folder (DSH grants each confined folder to the command and leaves that grant standing).

Asuna tools include scoped workspace operations, history search, structured discussion digest, `ask_character`, `report_progress`, and `read_image` when the action route declares image input. `read_image` is shared by both brains: her turn offers it when her own route declares image input and the conversation's recent pictures include one that can be pulled, when her turn has pictures it may send (`image_artifacts_from_program`), or in any owner-private conversation. Besides a message attachment ref, `read_image` takes the `artifact_id` of a stored image: one in the task's or turn's own scope, one she made herself (source `integration:`, from any scene), or one this turn was offered to send. Any other id answers `IMAGE_ARTIFACT_NOT_READABLE`, the same as an unknown one. This is how a picture is looked at again after it was drawn or before it is sent. Owner grants can add managed integration tools or self-development tools. A tool being registered does not grant it to every task. `import_integration_artifact` belongs to the managed integration group, not to the ordinary workspace group: a QQ task's grant never carries it. `generate_image` is granted to every task, from any conversation, whenever the integration is enabled and its profile has an endpoint named `image` (below).

`ask_character` asks her for a judgment that is hers and waits: her role session runs an internal turn (`think`, optionally `recall`, then `task` op `answer`) whose answer returns as the tool result. It creates no public reply, task or authority. `report_progress` leaves her a note without waiting; she reads it on her next turn.

## History, retrieval, and summaries

Conversation and memory records are scoped by scene and policy epoch. Every `recall` is audited (`recall.read`: what she asked (`query` may be left out when `sections` names what to read; then no memory search runs), the sections asked, read and missing, the memories returned, the turn's kind and place). In an owner-private conversation `recall` also returns up to three of her own thoughts (`kind` `monologue`) from her groups that match the question, as `own_thoughts_in_groups`, each with the group's name and when she thought it; never the groups' talk, and never in a group's own turn (`Retrieval.own_thoughts`, vector and lexical ranks fused, each re-read under the same filter). The vector index declares `kind` as a filter; an index made before that is updated at start, the old one serving meanwhile. Every recalled record says when it was said (`when`: the clock stamp her history lines use, of its line, a summary's last line or a thought, and how long ago, `ContextBuilder.when`). Retrieval leaves out what she still has in front of her: records from the conversation's delivered lines and her last three thoughts there (`RECENT_THOUGHTS`); a `recall` result counts the related ones it left out this way and says they are in the conversation in front of her, from the earliest one's time (`held_back`), and does not say she remembers nothing when they are all she has. Memory indexing runs in the background over each conversation's scope and, once a round, her owner-private memories, 16 units per embedding request. When the embedding service refuses a request (HTTP 400, for example a unit longer than the model's context), its units are embedded one by one: a refused unit is embedded from its opening 400, 200 or 100 characters (`embedding_opening_chars`), and one refused even then is marked `embedding_status: REFUSED` for that embedding route and found only by lexical recall. An unreachable service marks nothing. History search reads authorized inbound messages and delivered `SPEAK` messages; it can return literal matches and, when available, semantic candidates. It does not turn a derived summary into a verbatim quote.

The explicit `history` tool also reads `direction: archived` rows under `archive:<scene_id>` for the current scene and its configured read links, with the same epoch rules. Archive rows share the live scene's `scope_key`, so conversation erasure covers them. They have independent sequence numbers and retain original text and timestamps; empty text can represent a media-only source. A result marks them `archived: true` and uses the person's existing roster label or their stored historical name without creating a people entry. Use explicit `since` and `until` dates for older archives; `window_days` is limited to 90. Archive rows do not enter recent context, familiarity, attention, recovery, publication, chunking, summaries, or automatic memory retrieval. Groups without a live scene remain unreadable until admitted. Raw wire text, unresolved reply context and attachment metadata remain under `legacy`; they are not attributed to a speaker as new prose.

Structured discussion digests use the scene-bound live history reader, without archived rows. They report their actual time and source coverage, preserve source references, and identify partial results rather than claiming an unread window was fully covered.

Background dialogue summaries process new messages in direct-message and authorized-group scenes after the summary boundary is initialized. They include inbound messages and delivered public `SPEAK` messages, and do not block the conversation. Each summary stores its source IDs, source window, participants, and per-speaker attribution. The participant list is computed from stored authors, not inferred by the summarizing model. Correction annotations resolve only against records in the same scene and policy epoch; unresolved references remain unresolved. A summary batch states as corrections only lines whose target is known (a quoted line, or the author's own earlier line it points back to with a cue such as 刚才那句): a correction cue word alone lists nothing (`summary_attribution.corrections`). When a later correction points to an earlier summary, the host appends a correction marker without rewriting the earlier summary text. A summary is evidence for a person only when its recorded participants include that person.

## What a turn carries

The program adds most of a turn's context by itself, so it also bounds it (`context_budget.py`, versioned). Nothing is cut silently: what a turn leaves out is counted and said, and stays readable through `recall`.

The persona document supplies self-identity, embodiment and established biography. The common prompt keeps those
separate from shared conversation history, current scene facts and actual tool or delivery results. A persona's
body does not supply remote perception, establish physical co-presence or grant additional capabilities.

- **Per block.** Her mood lists every unsettled feeling and the strongest others up to 6, with `fainter` counting the rest; times are coarse words and event ids are 12-character prefixes (`feel` close/void accepts a prefix that names one event), so an unchanged mood reads the same from turn to turn. How to record a feeling is its own block, `how_to_record_from_program`. The clock is `clock_from_program`; how to write a plan is in the `plan` tool's description. Every time a model reads is on that clock (`local_time.py`): stored records keep UTC, and each turn context (`coordinator._deliver`), the gate's material, the action brain's program notes, and every role and action tool result pass `local_time.for_model` (the Host's `web_search` formats its publication times on the same clock), which rewrites each timestamp field (a key ending in `_at` or `_until`, or `at`, `until`, `since`, holding a full date-time) on one clock face in the conversation's time zone (`schedule_rules.scene_timezone`): `10/14 14:28`, with the year in front (`2025/9/27 14:00`) when it is another year or more than 90 days from the moment it is written (`local_time.face`); `clock_from_program.now_local` always carries the year. Free text is never rewritten; a sentence the program composes formats its own times (`local_time.face`), as the history search's lines do. The history search reads a `since` or `until` without an offset as a local time. Her ideas list open ideas before deferred ones (at most 20) and says how many more wait.
- **Her persona.** The system prompt (core header, persona and voice) is never cut. A section tagged `place:home`, `place:peer`, `place:group` or `place:dm` renders only in turns at those places (`visibility.place`: the owner's own conversations, a trusted home channel's line, a group, another direct conversation); a section without a place tag renders everywhere, and an unknown place is refused. The limit bounds the largest render over the places. Its limit is the larger of `render.budget_tokens` (default 6,144) and what the installed package's own persona and voice seeds render to plus a quarter, never above `render.max_window_share` (default 0.25) of the character route's context window. Tokens are estimated without a tokenizer: one per Chinese, Japanese or Korean character, a quarter per other character. Over the limit, the settings card and the memory tab show it in red, `render.over_budget` is audited, and an edit that would grow her persona or voice is refused (`PERSONA_RENDER_OVER_BUDGET`); edits that shrink it still apply. Seeds always load.
- **Her notes.** A note shown every turn (`inject: always` sections of a group's notes or a ledger, corrected sections left out) has a per-turn limit: 2,400 characters for a group's notes, 4,000 for each ledger. Within it the note shows whole, with `fullness` in words. Over it, the newest sections that fit show whole and the rest are named in `not_shown` by heading. A note more than twice its limit takes no new text (`NOTE_OVER_LIMIT`, said in words) until she shortens it, merges sections or tucks some away; shrinking edits always pass. Her understanding of a person, her character core and her current self are written whole and take at most 4,000 characters each. The persona and voice render has a fixed budget of 16,384 UTF-8 bytes by default (`render.budget_tokens`, which she may set up to 32,768).
- **Public words reach home only through her own review.** The recent experience a home heartbeat or self-improvement turn carries lists every line of her private conversations (`visibility.owner_private_scenes`) but only her own delivered lines from groups, other people's private chats and peer lines; a task from such a conversation keeps its headline, not its report. How the groups are going reaches home as words (`places_from_program`). Other people's words come home only through her ideas notebook (decided in self-improvement turns, with a reason) or a nightly `promote_memory`, which requires `why`, keeps it on the memory and audits `memory.promoted`. A memory she makes `public` is shown in every conversation, so it is refused (`PROMOTION_NOT_PUBLIC`) when any source turn was owner-private or any source memory is kept at home; it can stay `private`. Every other home turn carries `elsewhere_from_program` (`places.elsewhere`): per other conversation in the last 6 hours (groups, others' chats, and her other home lines marked （家里）), what she herself did there in words (how much she said, how many of her pictures and stickers she sent, the tasks she handed over and their state), never anyone's words. Home conversations are her local chat, the owner's direct chats, and conversations of a channel kind that declares `HOME = True` (`dsh-peer`: the owner's own agents); QQ and any other platform kind are outside. In a home turn someone wrote (`external`: the owner, or a peer on a home line; not her heartbeat, plans or nightly turns, which have `visit`) she may be sent on an errand: the turn carries `errand_places_from_program` (her unblocked groups and configured direct chats outside home, by name) and the `errand` tool (`place`, `request` ≤ 500 characters, optional `exactly`). It queues a `visit` input there with intent `errand` (`visit:errand:<scene>:<event>:<call>`, so two errands in one turn are two), up to 20 a day, outside the visit limits; the visit records who asked (`author`, and `by` when it was not the owner) and `errand.offered` audits it. That turn is public, sees only that chat and `visit_from_program.request`, named as whose words they are (`exactly` asks her to relay them prefixed 「<who>让我转告」, 主人 for the owner), and may stay silent. An errand may carry one of her own pictures (`artifact_id`; that turn's `image_artifacts_from_program` always lists it, as a visit's does). Pictures are offered only where the channel kind carries them (`TEXT_ONLY = True` on `dsh-peer`); a group or anyone's DM other than the owner's gets only pictures she made (`outbound_media.own_only`). A `share_picture` visit does not wait for a quiet room; `start_topic` does. Integration (LAN device) tools are granted to the owner in the local chat and in their own platform DM (`integration.owner_dm`, by route and canonical person). From a home turn her `plan` create may name a peer line (`line`, as listed in `lines_from_program`): the plan is stored, fires and is changed in that line (`from_scene_id` records where she made it), and home turns list it under that line as `your_plans_there`. Home turns then carry `errands_from_program`: where she went, what was asked, and a program state word — never what anyone there replied.
- **Her questions.** `questions.py` keeps one row per question in `questions` (owner-private scope). A home turn has `ask`: `do` `ask` takes `question` (≤ 800 characters, a question or a problem report), `to` (one to three of: this conversation, as 这里 or its place; a home place from `note_places_from_program`; 行动脑) and `patience` (一会儿 10 minutes, 一小时 the default, 半天, 一天, 回了为止 none); `read` takes `id` (one question with its answers' text, a task's report as its first page) or one place in `to` (her asks there in the last 30 days, at most 10, newest first: when, the state, minutes to the first answer, her `took`, and the words saying what was searched); `close` takes `id` and `took` (≤ 500 characters). Without `do`, `question` or `patience` means ask, `took` means close, anything else read. A place outside home is refused (`ASK_PLACE_NOT_HOME`), and so is the action brain where the turn cannot use `delegate` (`ASK_NO_ACTION_BRAIN`). Asking this conversation: her reply in that turn is the question. Asking another home place: a trusted `wake` note carries the question (`question` on the note; it is never a reply and does not count against the turn's notes, and the note lists leave it out), and her first line in the turn it opens is the question there. Asking the action brain: a `delegate` whose brief is the question. In a home place the answer is the first inbound line there after her question line that is not a program notice or an aside, unless lines name the question; on a line whose kind declares `MARKS_ANSWERS` (the agent line), the agent's own lines answer only by naming it (`channel_kinds.marks_answers`), the owner's still by coming first (`raw.asuna_answer.question`, kept only for a home kind: `tools/agent_line.py send --answers <id>`), which are its answers instead; the action brain's answer is its task's report once the task has ended (`tasks.TERMINAL`; `RETURNED` is the usual end), and a task that ended without one has not done it. A place states, in words: 还没问出来, 等着 (with `until`), 答了 (with `answered_at` and `by`), 没答，不等了 (patience passed; a later answer still joins), 没问（那一回合你没说） (the asking turn ended without a line), 没问出去 (her line failed), 行动脑在做, 行动脑没做完. The program closes a question once every place is settled — a home place or the action brain when it answered, a group when its patience is over (more of the group may answer until then), any place lapsed or never asked — or seven days after it was asked while a place without patience still waits; its `took` says how it ended (程序收的：…), and she replaces it by closing it herself. Every open question is brought up to date at each home turn, shown or not. Every home turn carries `questions_from_program`: her open questions (at most 8, newest first: `id`, the question's first 40 characters, `asked_at`, a state word, each place's state), and once in each home conversation a question the program closed, with its `took` (more than 8 waiting to be told: one line with how many and how each lot ended); a group's own block lists its newest 8 open there (`more` counts the rest) and tells each one closed there once too; in the turn a question's note opened, that question is named by its id only (the note carries its text). The Host gives an `ask` read result without the text of an answer the conversation still shows (by message id, or for a line written in the Web composer its event id), saying where it is. `close` names who answered; with tiered memory on it adds how to keep what she learned (`from` 别人说的, or a rewrite with 查过的 and its basis). A `close` on a closed question with a different `took` replaces it, keeping the close time (`question.retaken`), and says what it replaced. The audit records `question.asked`, `question.answered`, `question.lapsed` and `question.closed` without text. A question in a group is a `speak` with `wait=question` (and `patience`: 一会儿, the default, 半天 or 一天): its text parts are the question, and the group is its one place (`kind: group`). Within ten minutes of it, a person who writes there unaddressed gets her one look (`question_wait`), once each. Its answers are, in that group before its patience runs out, the replies to any of her question's lines, the lines that got her such a look, and reactions to her question line; each is kept by reference (who, when, the line), never its text, and stays recorded once joined. That group's turns carry `questions_from_program` for her open questions there: id, when she asked, the state with the minutes left, and who answered by label and time. She brings an answer home in her own words with `note`'s `question` in that group (a question asked there; anything else is refused, and so is `question` on a note written at home): the note (`answers` on the note row) shows at home with the program's line on who answered and when, and that the original lines are read with `ask` `read` and that id; home stores none of their text. That read gives each line's text from the group's record for that turn, marked as the group's own words (`where`); a line gone from the record reads 这条原话在群的记录里已经取不到了. Her messages in `developer_inbox` are moved into closed questions to the developer's line at her next home turn (`questions.migrate`, once each); the developer's line is the home route marked `"developer": true` in the channel settings, else the route named `claude-code`. `developer_inbox` keeps the program's own notes to the developer (`source.by: program`), which the developer reads (`developer_inbox.unread`) and marks `seen` or `answered` (`developer_inbox.mark`).
- **A Host taken away from outside.** A clean stop deletes the `host_leases` document; a Host killed from outside leaves this deployment's own lease behind. The next start finds it (`host_lease.DatabaseLease.left_behind`: the lease's expiry less its 90 seconds, so within one 30-second renewal of the last moment it was alive) and records one row in `host_stops` (`how` `killed`, `alive_until`, `back_at`). For 24 hours home turns carry `host_from_program`: 宿主上次是被外部终止的（不是正常停下）, when it was last known alive and when it came back on her clock face with the gap between, and a note that nothing ran in between and that nothing needs doing about it. Stopping a Host on purpose where a clean stop is not possible (a deploy that kills the process tree) is marked first with `python -m asuna.host_stops planned`: a stop within 10 minutes of the mark is recorded as `planned` and not shown.
- **What her night turns changed.** A home heartbeat (`presence`) carries `night_from_program` (`night.py`) when her night turns (`self_development` and `settlement`, by kind) changed something since the previous heartbeat started: `group_focus` active/rest, `set_policy`, `update_self`, `restart` request/drill/cancel, `plan` create/update/cancel, `peer_line` open/close, `ideas` review, `watch` on/off and `write_document`, each with her local time, what changed in words and the reason she gave (`why` or `reason`); `work_log` sections appear by name only (`work_log`). Refused calls and lookups do not count. At most 12 items, then a count. It is computed from the audit (`role_tool`, `turn.started`; indexed by type and time) and absent when nothing changed.
- **Her restarts.** Under the launcher's supervisor (`ASUNA_SUPERVISED=1` in the worker's environment), every home turn may use `restart`, whatever woke her: `preview` lists what a restart would interrupt now (the running and queued tasks, queued inputs, words not yet confirmed delivered, her plans due within 15 minutes, the QQ adapter, how long the last restart took); `request` and `drill` take `why` and `when` (`now`, `quiet` — the default, waiting for no running turn, task or queued input at most 30 minutes — or a local `HH:MM`, after which it waits for quiet the same way); `cancel` withdraws a request not yet carried out. One request waits at a time: a new one replaces it and its result says what it replaced (when, why, which conversation asked). A waiting request is kept in `<data>/restart/pending.json`, so a restart by someone else before it is due keeps it, and every home turn shows it in `restart_from_program` (`waiting`: which of her conversations asked, when it goes, why). When it is due, the worker marks the stop planned and writes `<data>/restart/request.json` with what it interrupts (`restarts.Desk`, a 15-second tick); `restart.requested` audits it. A `drill` has the supervisor fail its first start on purpose. Without the supervisor the request is refused (`RESTART_NOT_SUPERVISED`). For 24 hours after a restart, home turns carry `restart_from_program` from the supervisor's `<data>/restart/last.json`: who asked and why, when it stopped and came back, the result (running the version due / returned to the previous version that ran), at a restart she asked for what became of the checkout (`checkout`: fast-forwarded to `origin/main`, or one line why not), each start (with its exit code when the Host exited), what loaded, the tasks left paused by it, and whether the QQ adapter's health file was written since. A restart that returned to a previous version queues one home `presence` turn at once (`restart.called`).
- **Errands.** A home turn may use `errand` when someone speaks there, when her plan comes due there, or when a note opens it: a note of hers from home, or a note from outside that carries the owner's own line (`notes.owner_line`: the note was written in a turn woken by a platform line whose account maps to the owner; `note_from_program.owner_said` shows it). `errand` takes `place` (from `errand_places_from_program`: her groups and the direct chats configured for her, never a home conversation), `request` (≤ 500 characters), `exactly` and an optional `artifact_id` of a picture she made; at most 20 a day. The errand waits in that conversation's queue; a turn that comes up more than 20 minutes after it was queued is not run (`ERRAND_LATE`), and one a restart cut is not run again (`ERRAND_INTERRUPTED`: what it already sent stands). The turn runs in its own session, `asuna-errand-<hash>`: the Host copies the conversation's events up to its last completed turn (DSH's fork seed), so the system prompt and tool list are the conversation's own; when the conversation has no completed turn, is in a turn, or would come within 16,384 tokens of its compaction threshold, the session starts empty and the turn's `delivered_history` brings the conversation's recent lines (none is shown there yet). In that turn the tools the conversation lists act with home's authority (`RoleTools._cls`): home-scope `recall` alongside the conversation's own, home documents through `write_document`, path reads through `read_image`; her thoughts are kept in the home turn's scope. What goes out there and who is there stay the conversation's: pictures follow the conversation's picture rules, `watch`, `note` and blocking work as in its own turns, and `delegate`, `task` `message` and `task` `stop` are refused (a task outlives the turn). `visit_from_program` carries the request and `carries`; every tool result ends with `turn` (or the refusal with the same words in brackets). Her lines sent there are marked `errand` (history search shows `errand`, the relevance gate reads 你（差事）, `elsewhere_from_program` counts them). The conversation's next turn of its own carries `errand_from_program` once: who sent her from home and what she said there, never the request. When the errand turn ends its session is archived; the launcher moves it out of the store after a day and drops it after 14. Home sees the outcome in `errands_from_program`.
- **Notes between her own conversations.** `notes.py` keeps one row per note in `notes`. She writes one with `note` in any turn that lists note places (`note_places_from_program`; never `consult`). It takes `to` (a place from `note_places_from_program`, or 家里 from outside), `text` and `deliver`: at most 1,500 characters from home to another home line (`notes.HOME_NOTE_CHARS`), otherwise at most 300 (`notes.NOTE_CHARS`); one over its length is refused (`NOTE_TOO_LONG`) with three ways on: shorten it, split it into two marked 1/2 and 2/2, or send the cut version the refusal gives. The program stamps the trust from the sending turn's session class: `trusted` from home, `untrusted` from outside. Home reaches its other home lines (`wake` by default, or `next_time`), any unblocked group (`wake` by default, or `next_time`; `wake` counts as a visit with intent `note` under `places.eligibility` and is recorded in the presence plan) and the direct chats an errand can reach (`next_time` only). Outside reaches home — the local chat (what 家里 names) and the owner's own direct chats, never a peer or agent line — and other groups, all as `wake`. A channel line whose adapter marks it `raw.asuna_aside` (a peer's turn that did not answer her, `packages/channels/dsh-peer`) is stored like an unwoken group line, `processing_outcome: RECORDED_NO_WAKE`, without a turn; her home turns list a peer line's asides since she last spoke on it (`peer_asides_from_program`, `lines.asides`: time and an 80-character excerpt, the newest 6 and how many more), and a note to that line is how she answers. A note from outside sharing a 20-character run (whitespace ignored) with someone else's line in that conversation from the last 2 hours is refused (`NOTE_NOT_OWN_WORDS`). The limits are 2 notes a turn, 6 a day from one public conversation to each home conversation, and 3 a day per pair of groups with 10 in all. Every refusal is `note.refused` and every note `note.sent` in the audit, with no text. A `wake` note to home queues the internal input `note` (`note:<scene>:<note>`, authorized as the scene's own person and allowed only in owner-private scenes); one to a group queues a `visit` with intent `note` (`visit:note:<scene>:<note>`, a scene tick with no channel envelope). Both go through `Chat.offer_internal`, so the input is durable before it is queued, recovered at restart, deduplicated, and runs after everything that scene already has queued. Nothing is appended to a running session. `note` is a core notice kind and its input is authored `asuna:internal`. The turn it opens carries `note_from_program`: where and when she wrote it, the trust words, the text, and a fixed note (from outside to home, the caution that it is a message and nothing with consequences happens in this turn). Its trigger is `note`. A home `note` turn drops the relationship, sender and understanding blocks. A trusted one has the tools of a scheduled turn there, without `visit`. An untrusted one has only `think`, `recall`, `read_image` (when she has pictures), `stay_silent`, `ideas` note, `feel`, `watch`, `speak` where the conversation speaks in parts (the owner's platform chat) and a reply by `note` to the sending conversation. A turn opened by a note may reply once (`hop` 2, `reply_to`); the reply's turn may not write another. Every turn of a conversation lists notes written to it in the last 72 hours (`notes_from_program`, newest 5). An unread note shows its full text; a read one shows a 60-character excerpt. A note from outside shows only a summary in a home `self_development` turn, the turn that holds the development grant; every other home turn shows its text with the caution. Every turn also lists the notes written from there (`notes_sent_from_program`) with a state word: 送到了，还没读 / 读了，没回 / 读了，回了你 / 三天没读，过期了. A note counts as read once a finished turn has shown it. Public platform turns carry one sentence in `speak_from_program.one_self`. At home, a reason she recorded with `feel` outside says where (`where`), and so does a watch reason written outside. An idea in her notebook names the conversation it came from (`where`). A home turn may `feel` close or void a feeling recorded in a group. `delegate` and `task` op `message` are refused after 6 hand-offs in a row with nobody speaking (`delegation_depth`).
- **Nightly tidying.** Her settlement turn carries `notes_review_from_program` when notes are due: over or near their limit, or changed and not tidied for 7 days, at most 3 a night, most pressing first, each with its sections. Tidying is hers (rewrite, merge, tuck away, or leave as is); the program never edits or deletes her text.
- **The whole turn.** The ceiling holds for what a turn sends, not for what the worker prepares: in her native session the Host applies it to the notice it composes, after leaving out what the session still shows (`context_budget.ceiling` gives it the table; `context-delivery.js` `trimTurn`), and a context sent whole is held to it by the worker on its way out. Past 48,000 JSON characters the oldest rows of a few lists go first — members, recent experience, promotion candidates, recalled memories, the topic thread, then history down to its last 4 lines. If the turn is still over (one huge message, row or block), the ceiling is hard: the longest text left is halved, repeatedly and never below 400 characters, keeping its start and saying how many characters went and that the original stays readable through recall. Under the ceiling nothing is cut. `trimmed_from_program` says what was left out or shortened; a row, memory or item left out, and a block with a text cut, no longer count as carried. The worker records the prepared context's size as `context_chars` in its manifest; the turn's `turn.output` audit carries what the Host left out (`delivery.trimmed`), and a context sent whole audits its trim as `context.trimmed`.
- **A conversation that no longer fits.** When a character turn in a scene's main conversation still fails with DSH's `CONTEXT_WINDOW_EXCEEDED` after DSH's own overflow compaction, the plugin reports it with the session's last `compaction/summary` text (`carry`), and the worker (`BusinessWorker.carry_session`) continues the scene in a new session: the scene gets a new `character_context` (`carried-<hex>`), the new main binding records `carry` (`from_session_id`, `summary`, `delivered`), and the old binding is retired with an explicit `character_context` and `successor_id`; `native.context.carried` is audited. The Host request `carry_session` prepares the new session as navigation does (title, workspace, nothing of the old session seeded), announces it to the sidebar and archives the old one once its agent is idle. Everything that resolves a conversation through `continued_session` follows the chain of successors; an action session started before the switch keeps its parent. The turn runs again once in the new session (`turn.carried`, with a resume note saying why), and its first notice carries `carried_from_program`: the summary, cut at 12,000 characters with the ceiling's marker, and a note that the earlier conversation no longer fit, that recent lines are in `delivered_history` and older ones in recall (without a summary, only the note). It is sent once, marked delivered when that stage succeeds. A carried session whose first notice never got through is not carried again: the turn fails as before.

## People in conversations

Identity is the account: an author's `person_id`, mapped to its canonical person by `canonical_persons`. That mapping is read from configuration only, so removing an alias immediately ends owner-private access for that entry point. A display name never decides who someone is. Each person gets a fixed label in each scene the first time they appear (`[name #n]`, stored in `scene_people`). The number never changes, so renames, members who share a name, and compaction neither merge nor split people. Names come from the verified platform profile on that person's own messages; a name the operator set in `identities.display_name` takes precedence for everyone except the owner. Names are NFKC-normalized, reduced to one line of visible characters, and stripped of the characters labels are built from (`[ ] 【 】 「 」 『 』 # < >`).

In the character's conversation, a QQ message is its label line followed by the message text indented by two spaces, one line per line break of any kind (`\r`, U+2028 and the like included); a text line that starts with `>` shows it as `＞`, so only the program's own lines start with `>`; label-shaped text someone sent (`[name #4]`, `@#4`) reads as `［name ＃4］` and `@＃4`, in their lines, quoted replies, the turn's own event text, correction snippets and what summaries read, so only the program's labels have that form. Where the channel declares `quote` or `quote_in`, each line's label line ends with its number in that conversation, `〔n〕` (`scene_seq`: a line's identity there, given in the order lines are stored, not a time). A quote, anyone's, adds an indented `> 回复 〔n〕 <speaker> <time>：<first words>` line (her own lines as `你`; no number where the channel shows none). Every line something points at — a quote, a notice, her own receipts, the recent lines a wrong number lists — has this one form: number, speaker and time as its own label line has them (her clock; a caught-up line's original time), then its first 40 characters with `…` when longer (`People.pointed`, `POINTED_CHARS`); a quote of a line the program has no record of reads `> 回复了一条更早的消息`, and a line that only quotes reads `（只引用了这条，没写字）`. Under a line of hers that someone else points at — a quote, or a notice about it (a reaction, a recall, an essence), the notices folded at her next turn included — comes `> （你说那句时心里想的：…）`: her thought from the turn that said it, cut where a sentence ends within about 60 characters, with `…（截断，原文 N 字）` when cut. It comes once a turn however many lines point at the same one (the relevance gate's own view always has it), never under anyone else's line, and never under her own acts. History rows give the quoted line as `quotes` in the same form. The `speak` result gives each sent line's number there, and a text part containing `〔n〕` is refused (`编号是挑引用用的，不写进你说的话里。`). An @mention of a known person reads as that person's label, and a mention of her own account reads as her name. After the label, the program notes in words: a group owner or admin role, another member with the same or a look-alike name, a name containing the owner's label or her own name, and a rename within the last 24 hours. The owner's label starts with the persona's `people.owner_label` (core default `本机用户`) and is decided by account. Context blocks name authors by `speaker` label instead of author ids, and a recalled summary gives `who`, `about_current_speaker` and correction sentences. The summarizing model receives the same labels. QQ numbers stay in program data. Her context names conversations instead of giving their ids: `scene` is 家里·私聊, 群聊「name」 or 私聊 with the person's label, and a row or memory from another conversation carries that conversation's name (a shared memory reads 不分场合, an owner-only one 只在私下). Scene ids, scope keys, policy epochs and platform message numbers are not in her context.

To @ someone in a group she writes their label (`@[name #4]`, or `@#4`; the owner's label may start with the owner word, and the whole label may sit in one more pair of brackets). The outbox turns it into the adapter's `@qq:<account>` marker when the item leaves the host; the stored message keeps the label, and a label that names nobody in the scene is sent as a plain `@name`. Two things in her speech are handed back in the same turn (`people.speech_problem`, detection only, nothing rewritten): a label opening a line without @ (开头的「[name #4]」不会 @ 到人…), and a label's number outside a whole label when it is someone in that scene (`@name #4`, `name #4`, a bare `#4`: 「#4」是程序给你认人的编号，群里的人看不到…, with that person's whole label to copy for an @ and the advice to drop # when she did not mean a person). A number that is nobody there, such as an issue number, passes. No label leaves the program: one written without @ whose number is someone in the scene is sent as their shown name (the name she wrote when the platform gives none, a real @ when neither is known); bracketed text whose number is nobody there is not a label and is sent as written (`people.outbound`). A stored `@qq:<account>` reads back to her as the label.

The history and discussion-digest tools take `person` as a label, a `#number`, an id, or a name. A name resolves against the scene's people (current, former and operator-given names, and names shown on their own verified messages); when it matches several people the tool answers with their labels and runs no query, and the filter is always by author, never by a name on a message. Results name writers by label and carry no author or person ids.

How well she knows the current speaker reaches her in `relationship` as words: `familiarity` is one level (the owner; known, when she has written an understanding of them or answered them many times; regular; met; new), computed from the owner's account, her written understanding, the turns in which she answered them and the lines they wrote, across every conversation of the canonical person. Nothing a message says raises it. A persona words each level and gives its `stance` in `people.familiarity`. What she has written about them comes in two layers (`understanding.py`). `person` is one record per canonical person (`relationship:<person>|person`), read in every conversation where she meets them: `first` always says 这只是他的一部分，不是他的全部 and, when she keeps a here layer for them in other conversations, how many; `text` is what she wrote; `written` names the conversation it was last written in and how long ago (each revision keeps `origin`: scene and time). `here` is the record for this person in this conversation (`relationship:<person>|scene:<scene>`, shared with a configured linked entry as before), read only there. No record is seeded: everyone starts with none, which reads as 你还没写过这个人在哪儿都成立的那一层 and 你还没写过他在这里是什么样. `understand_person` takes `layer` (`person` or `here`, required; `UNDERSTANDING_LAYER_REQUIRED`) and the layer's whole new `content`; each layer is written at most once a turn (`understanding_update`, `person_understanding_update`), from any conversation, with the same source registration. The program does not filter what goes where: `understanding_update_from_program.target` gives her three questions to ask first (does it hold in another group; would saying it somewhere proper make the person uncomfortable; did they say it, did she see it, or does she infer it). Erasing a memory takes back every person-layer revision citing a source from that conversation; the record falls back to the newest revision before it, or is removed.

## Her place in a group

Her own role in each group reaches her in words in `your_place_from_program`: 群主, 管理员, 普通成员, or not yet known. The adapter asks the platform for the logged-in account's own role (cached for ten minutes) and sends it in `raw.asuna_self`. The host keeps only a valid role, and only on group events.

Where she is the group's owner or an admin, the block also tells her how to use her `group_action` tool:

- `kind`: `mute`, `unmute`, `recall` or `kick`;
- `who`: a label or `#n` (not for a recall, whose line names whose it is);
- `duration`: a whole number of minutes, 1 to 43,200 (mute only);
- `line`: the line to recall, by its number or `这条`, as a quote names one (recall only);
- `reason`.

The program refuses any of these, by name:

- she is not an admin;
- the route sets `admin_actions: false` (admin actions are on by default);
- the target is unclear or unknown;
- the target is the owner, herself, the group's owner or another admin;
- a mute without a duration, or one that is not a whole number of minutes between 1 and 43,200;
- a recall whose `line` is no line of that group (`GROUP_ACTION_LINE`, with the last lines listed), or is hers.

An accepted action becomes a `group_action` artifact. The outbox hands it to the adapter before any message, and the adapter calls `set_group_ban`, `delete_msg` or `set_group_kick`. The platform's answer arrives through the same receipt endpoint and updates the artifact (DONE, FAILED or UNKNOWN). An admin action has no message of its own, so its receipt carries no `platform_message_id` and is not held to the requirement a message receipt has; how often she acts in one group the program does not cap. An action interrupted while it was being sent becomes UNKNOWN and is never retried. Her words for the result say 已交给平台，等确认 until the platform answers; the account and message numbers stay in program data.

`group_notes_from_program` shows her own notes about the group: who is who, its customs, how she acts there. She writes them from that group's own turns with `write_document` and `doc: "group_notes"` (`append_section`, `replace_section`, or `set_tags` to tuck a section away with `inject: "on_demand"`). The program maps that name to the group's document and keeps its sections public to that group, also when she tidies them from home by the document name her nightly settlement lists. The block shows the notes within their per-turn limit (see What a turn carries).

Her `work_log` is a running record (`documents.JOURNALS`): she reads and writes it like any document (`write_document` `append_section`, `replace_section`, `set_tags`; a section read by `recall` with its `section`), but it is stored one row per entry (collection `journal_entries`), each change kept beside its entry (`journal_revisions`), so a write touches only its entry. `correction` and seeds do not apply to it. In any document, the `section` a `replace_section`, `correction` or `set_tags` names is found by its id, by its heading as written, or by a heading whose slug no other section shares; otherwise it is refused (`DOC_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`) with the ids there are. Its sections are `on_demand` and `owner_private` unless she says otherwise.

The `append_section` that creates a document may say its `kind`: `ledger` (its `inject: always` sections reach every turn where they are readable, as `ledgers_from_program`; new sections default to `always`, and `correction` applies), `journal` (a running record stored as rows, like `work_log`) or `working` (the default; read by `recall`). The kind is chosen once: a `kind` on a later write that differs from the document's is refused (`DOC_KIND_FIXED`), and one outside the three is refused (`DOC_KIND_INVALID`). In a group's notes `kind` is ignored.

nothing below is read or written, and her prompts, tools and turns are as described elsewhere in this document.

program has not seen takes its place from its `inject` (`always`, `on_demand`, `never`) with a fresh stay; from then

`on_demand` or `never`, at that place). `when_shown` `on_demand` or `never` on an item lets it go to that place; `inject:
always` never moves an item up, and its result says how one comes back (used again, rewritten with a change, or

from its last memorable moment (group notes 28, `core` and voice 56; on the day the switch goes on, each first stay is

recently memorable one (one written in a turn where she recorded a feeling last), named under `moved`. Each item is a fact (事实) or a memory (记忆): by default
facts in persona, ledgers, group notes and `core`, memories in `self`, voice and running records; `write_document`

## Shadow pilot of her own items

`memory_pilot.py` measures which of her own items a turn would surface, and delivers nothing. Her persona sections, the list lines of `current_self` (30 characters or longer), the rows of each running record and each group's notes sections are embedded on the memory embedding route into the collection `memory_shadow` (rows `item`), apart from `memory_units`; an owner-private item is readable only in owner-private turns, a public one everywhere, a group's notes only in that group. The items are read again at most every ten minutes, and only new or changed ones are embedded. After each character turn's context is prepared, the turn is queued; a background thread scores the items against the turn's query the way retrieval scores memories (vector and lexical ranks fused; the vector score is the index's (1 + cosine) / 2, reusing the vector the turn's retrieval computed), takes at most two above the floor of 0.83 (`FLOOR`, `OWN_SLOTS`), and writes one row (`turn`, or `recall` for a `recall` query): item ids, stores and scores, the best six candidates above 0.75, the turn's kind, the conversation and its kind, the query's hash, the time the job took. An item a turn would surface records the conversation in `surfaced_in`. Pairs of items in one store scoring 0.93 or more are written as `pair` rows. No text, heading or query is written, and nothing of it reaches a prompt. `tools/memory_pilot_report.py` prints the daily reading in counts; `--drop` removes the collection. The switch is `memory_pilot.SHADOW_PILOT`: off, nothing is logged, and the Host runs the item index only for tiered
memory when that is on (its items come up from these vectors whatever the pilot switch says).

## Media and image reading

An adapter may normalize non-text segments into bounded `raw.asuna_media` metadata. The host preserves placeholders and does not treat them as visual input. A merged forward's messages may ride as `raw.asuna_forward` (`forwards.py`): the host keeps at most 3 blocks of at most 100 messages of 500 characters, each message's text on one line and its sender's name cleaned like any shown name, each block with a `ref` (`f` and 8 hex digits). The line's text shows the count, the ref and the first messages; `recall` with a section `{doc: "forward", sid: <ref>}` reads the forward in full, 4,000 characters a page (`<ref>:2` for the next, named in the result's `next`), only in the conversation it arrived in. The action brain's history query matches a received line by its text or its forwarded messages and shows those under the line. The action task receives a bounded list of image attachments from its own scene and policy epoch, plus images from scenes it may read only through a configured cross-scene read link; every entry carries `scene_id` and `linked_scene`, so the listing states where each image came from.

The action brain can call `read_image({"ref": "…", "max_bytes": 4194304})` when the action brain's model takes images (its input types in DSH's catalog). The reference selects an attachment already visible to that task; it cannot widen the read scope. A turn's listing shows the newest pictures, while a reference copied earlier is found among the inbound pictures of the last 7 days (`vision.REF_DAYS`) in the same scenes (`vision.attachment_by_ref`; `sticker` `keep` and `remember` look refs up the same way). In an owner-private conversation and the tasks it hands over, a reference from any of her conversations is found too and read back in its own scene (`home_reach`); a public conversation never reaches another's. That scope is recomputed from configuration at call time (`context_links` or route-level `read_scenes`, own scene first), so deleting the configured edge returns to own-scene-only, and a linked scene missing from the database or sitting in another policy epoch is simply not scanned. Bytes pulled from a linked scene are still stored under the calling task's own scope key. URL fetching requires an allowed host and safe redirect, enforces a byte limit, and accepts PNG, JPEG, WebP, or GIF data identified by file signature. An animated picture (GIF, WebP or APNG with more than one frame) reaches the model as a PNG contact sheet (`animated.py`): the moments at even intervals across one loop (`CELLS`, 9), neighbouring moments that look the same merged into one cell, each cell labelled with its order and its time in the animation; transparent pixels show light grey. The result's `animated` words say how many frames there are, how long one loop takes and whether it repeats, how much the picture moves, how many of the frames left out differ visibly from their nearest cell, whether there is fast back-and-forth flicker (frames of at most 60 ms alternating), a frame that holds most of a loop, and that the sheet is not the animation. The stored bytes stay the original. One too large to split (over 1,500 frames or 300 million decoded pixels) or that fails to split is passed as it is, and `animated.error` says only its first frame is seen. A video's reference (`（视频 ref：att-…）` under its line) is read the same way: the video is downloaded into memory (at most 32 MB, `vision.VIDEO_MAX_MB`; one whose platform size is larger is not pullable, and the refusal names both sizes), cut at once into the same kind of contact sheet by seeking 36 even moments (`animated.video_snapshot`: every fourth is a cell, the rest measure motion and what the cells miss), and dropped; only the sheet is stored and seen. Its words also say how long the video is, whether it has sound, and that the sheet has none. A video that cannot be split, a missing decoder (`av`) or a failed download each returns its reason, never an empty picture. An optional local image directory can also be configured. Pulled bytes are stored through the scoped blob store and passed to DSH as a durable image attachment. Stored pictures live by their kind (`media_life.POLICY`, swept when the worker starts and then daily): a pulled copy (`read_image`, a video's sheet, a picture read from disk) is kept for 7 days, as long as its reference is accepted, unless her shelf, the sticker pool or a sent message names it; a sticker pool candidate is kept while the pool or her shelf lists it; her records (kept stickers, pictures she made or edited, pictures the owner sent at home) are kept. The stored tool receipt contains metadata and blob references, not base64 image bytes.

The character brain has the same tool. Her turn offers `read_image` when the character brain's model takes images and the conversation's recent pictures — the same window, fences and linked scenes the action brain reads — include one that can be pulled. The call runs in her turn's scene and scope, stores bytes the same way, and becomes an image attachment in her turn; the episode's `tool_calls` and the audit keep only the receipt. A full group turn brings pictures by itself (`role_tools.turn_pictures`): the pictures on the line that started it, then photos (not stickers) from the 6 lines before it, at most 2 in all; each is pulled like `read_image` and becomes an image part of the turn's first message, and `pictures_from_program` names each `ref` and its line (触发这一回合的那条 / 前面第 n 条; an animated one also carries its `animated` words). One that cannot be pulled is left out. A platform line entering her conversation names its pullable pictures under the text (`（图 ref：att-…）`), and her media note tells her to look herself, or to put the `ref` in the brief when she hands picture work to the action brain. Looking does not wait on sending: in an owner-private turn where none of her pictures can be sent (her local chat has no channel route), her newest own pictures — at most 5, ids and when — are listed as `your_pictures_from_program`, and `read_image` comes with them. A picture the model refuses does not end the turn: DSH stores a looked-at picture in the conversation's session log and rebuilds every request from it, so when the provider refuses a request from one of Asuna's sessions as invalid (`INVALID_REQUEST`, e.g. a WebP its server cannot decode) while the conversation still sends pictures, the plugin records one DSH `image/offload` decision for every retained picture there — they become DSH's "image omitted" placeholders from then on — and the request is tried once more (`image-refusal.js`). A refusal with no picture left to omit ends the turn with the provider error.

The default byte limit equals the 8 MiB hard limit: QQ photos are routinely 4-6 MiB and DSH re-encodes request images toward a 1 MiB target, so blocking at pull time would be an Asuna fence, not a route capability. Host allowlists, timeout, local directories, and insecure HTTP policy are configured under `vision`; the allowlist defaults to the multimedia host this deployment has actually received. Unsupported routes, disallowed sources, and fetch failures return real error codes, and a non-2xx response carries the server's bounded reason with any temporary `rkey` redacted; placeholders are not reported as viewed images. Download URLs are re-read from the stored message at pull time and are never embedded in the listing or truncated. A link its platform signs (`channel_kinds.media_key_name`) is signed again with the current key of its name before the fetch (`vision.media_source`), so a picture days old is still read; a refused signed link says which of three it is: `IMAGE_LINK_EXPIRED` (the current key was put in and the host still says expired), `IMAGE_GONE` (the host says the picture is gone, HTTP 404 or 410) or `IMAGE_SIGNATURE_UNAVAILABLE` (no current key was known; worth trying again in a few minutes). Durable image references enter the native attachment pipeline; request image limits and pricing belong to the selected DSH provider. There is no Asuna token proxy.

A picture the owner attaches in her local chat stays in her conversation, so a character route that declares image input sees it directly; past the route's image budget, DSH's image offload — mounted once by its base profile for every session; a second mount in a preset breaks session resume — swaps the oldest for placeholders and retries. The plugin also reads each attached picture back from DSH's attachment store (at most 8 per input) and passes it with the input; the host stores it as an image artifact in the local chat's scope (source `local-upload:<input id>`, once per input) and records it on the input row as `raw.asuna_media` with `origin: local_upload`, its placeholder leading the text — so history and recall keep it after compaction, a picture-only message is a valid input, and a picture that could not be read is recorded as such. Her turn's media note says the picture is already in the conversation; the action brain reads it by `ref` from the blob store (`pulled_via: blob`, no host allowlist, nothing stored twice). Only the program's own local input can name such an artifact: a platform message carrying the same fields is not pullable from the blob store. As her image in the local chat it can be attached in the owner's linked QQ DM, never in a group.

### Sending an image with her words

A turn may also carry an image out. When the scene is the owner's own private conversation (`session_class=owner_private`) and its channel route targets a `dm`, the context gains `image_artifacts_from_program`: the image artifacts this turn may reference, each with `artifact_id`, `sha256` and `size`, plus a note that these are program-held artifacts and not file paths. Only then does her turn offer the `attach_image` tool (`artifact_id`, `why`): one picture per turn — calling it again replaces the first — and only an `artifact_id` from that list. A direction this version does not open, an artifact that was not offered this turn, bytes that are not an image by file signature, or a size over 8 MiB is refused as a tool error with its code and reason, and she can still finish the turn. A turn that ends with `stay_silent` and has no words sends no picture. In a group she is in (a group scene with a group route), the list holds only pictures she produced herself — bytes from her own generator (`generate_image` or `import_integration_artifact`, source `integration:`), from whichever of her scenes they were made in — and her edits of pictures that came from that conversation, never a picture someone sent her; she decides whether and where to send one, always all-ages. The tool and the attachment endpoint refuse anything else in a group with `ATTACHMENT_NOT_HER_OWN`. Other people's private conversations, and a private scene routed to a group, are refused deterministically instead of silently degrading into text whose receipt reports a picture as delivered.

Her credentials (`credentials.py`): a credential is a name, a note and up to 6 environment variables. Values live in DSH's credential store, one record `asuna-cognition-core/<name>` (kind `grant`, payload `{note, env}`), read and written by the Host on the worker's `credentials` request. At home (owner-private `external` and `task_feedback` turns) the character brain's `credential` tool files one (`keep` with `name`, `note`, `env`; again under the same name to replace it) or removes it (`drop`); the value passes her model in that call only. Home turns and home tasks list `credentials_from_program` / `facts.credentials`: names, notes and variable names, never a value. Only a task from home may use one: `sandbox_run` with `credentials` (names) sets those variables for that one command (`CREDENTIALS_HOME_ONLY` otherwise). Every stored value, as written or URL-encoded, is replaced by `[凭据已隐藏]` in tool results and receipts, in her briefs and messages to the action brain and in its reports. A script that holds a value could still send it out over the network; the protection is that no model holds it. The core skill `asuna-credentials` teaches the rules, including using someone's account only for what it was granted for.

Stickers and the platform's own faces. A sticker is a picture someone sent *as a sticker* — still or animated, or a store sticker — never a photo; the adapter marks it on the media item (`sticker: custom|market`), and for items stored before it did, the platform's channel kind tells from the item (`sticker_of`). A platform line names its stickers under the text as `（表情包 ref：att-…）` and its other pictures as `（图 ref：…）`. In a conversation whose platform sends stickers (`channel_kinds.sends_stickers`), her turn carries `stickers_from_program` — her shelf by name, each with her note on when it fits, how often she has used it in words, the shelf's count and fullness word — and `faces_from_program` — the face names people there used lately — and offers the `sticker` tool: `keep` (a sticker `att-` ref from this conversation, or the `blob-` id of a picture she produced; a `name` of up to 12 characters and a `when` of up to 40), `drop` and `rename`. A photo is refused (`STICKER_IS_A_PHOTO`), so are a name or sticker she already keeps (the same picture with other metadata counts as kept, `STICKER_ALREADY_KEPT`), and a full shelf (`STICKER_SHELF_FULL`, 60, `context_budget.STICKER_SHELF`). Keeping a custom sticker pulls its bytes once into `global-safe` (source `sticker:<message>`); a store sticker keeps the platform ids it is sent again by. To send one she writes `[表情包:名字]` in her speech: it leaves as a message of its own, one per turn, its row keeping the token as text and carrying `sticker` (and, for a custom one, the picture as `attachment`, which the attachment endpoint serves to any conversation that takes stickers because it is on her shelf). `[表情:名字]` inside her words is a platform face; the adapter turns a known name into the real face. Her speech is sent back in the same turn when it names a sticker she does not keep, more than one sticker, a face name the platform does not know, or either where stickers cannot go (her local chat). At home `stickers_from_program` lists the shelf for tidying in the turns that tidy it (self-development, a self-evaluation or dice-review heartbeat) and only the shelf's names in other home turns, with `sticker` `do: shelf` listing it whole on asking; each sticker with a picture carrying its `ref` (its artifact id), which `read_image` takes, and the nightly settlement carries `stickers_review_from_program` once a week (or whenever the shelf is 80% full): the stickers kept two weeks and never sent, or not sent for two weeks, at most 6. Rotating is hers — the program never drops one. The candidate pool: every sticker posted in a group is saved as it arrives (`stickers.pool_seen`, off the input path; bytes in `global-safe`, source `sticker-pool:<message>`) into `sticker_pool`, merged by picture or store id with how often and where it was seen, skipping what is on her shelf; past `POOL_MAX` (30) the least recently seen leave. Her nightly settlement carries `sticker_candidates_from_program` (the 8 most posted) and a group turn the ones posted there in the last 2 hours (4); wherever her shelf is, `sticker` `pool` returns the same list as the nightly one with the pool's count, and the turn then offers `read_image`. Each `candidate` is an artifact id she may `read_image`. `sticker` `keep` with `candidate` puts one on her shelf only if she looked at it in that turn (`STICKER_CANDIDATE_NOT_LOOKED`), under her name and note; it then leaves the pool. Stickers she knows: `sticker_memory` keeps, per persona and for good, what she called a sticker, keyed by a fingerprint — a store sticker's `market:<emoji_id>`, or `md5:<hex>` of a picture's bytes, which the channel kind can read from a media item without fetching it (`channel_kinds.sticker_md5`; QQ names the file by it) — and by its picture (`picture`: `img:` and the SHA-256 of its image data without metadata, `stickers.picture_key`; JPEG APP segments and comments, PNG text, time, EXIF and pHYs chunks, WebP EXIF, XMP and VP8X chunks are left out). A posted custom sticker whose md5 is unknown is downloaded once by the program as its line enters her conversation (`PICTURE_FETCH_SECONDS`, 5 s; a failure leaves it unknown) and matched by its picture; a match adds that md5 to the memory's `aliases`, so a copy re-sent with rewritten metadata is known and the next one needs no download. Every sticker she keeps is known by its name and note (rename updates it; dropping it does not forget it; at startup `remember_shelf` covers the shelf as it is, pictures included), and `sticker` `remember` (an `att-` ref or a `candidate`, with `name` and `when`) records one she looked at in that turn and does not want to keep (`STICKER_NOT_LOOKED`, `STICKER_NO_FINGERPRINT`); remembering again replaces her words. When a known sticker is posted, its line reads `（表情包 ref：att-…，你认得：「名字」，她的说明；在你架子上|你收过又放下了|你看过没收）`, also when the picture itself can no longer be pulled. A known sticker is never a pool candidate, and a candidate she remembers leaves the pool. `read_image` of a sticker adds a `sticker_note` pointing at `remember` and `keep`. A claim that does not declare `supports=sticker` gets no sticker row: it is failed with `CHANNEL_DOES_NOT_DECLARE_STICKER` rather than sent half.

Two things put images on that list: bytes this scene already stored (`read_image`, and `import_integration_artifact` when the imported bytes turn out to be an image), and images registered in **one other scene belonging to the same person**. That exception exists because an import runs only in the local owner task (and a picture is often made there) while the turn that speaks the picture is usually the QQ private chat, and both entries are the owner's own private space. `outbound_media.image_scopes` computes the readable set from configuration at read time: this turn must be `owner_private`, the peer scene must be a `dm` whose canonical person is the same owner, and the two scenes must be joined by a `context_links` / route `read_scenes` edge. Between two owner-private scenes of one person the edge counts in either direction — neither end can downgrade owner-private data into a public session, which is what `visibility.without_link_downgrades` exists to refuse. Groups, another person's scenes, unlinked scenes and public turns never extend the list, and offered items that came from the peer scene say so (`from_linked_scene`, plus its `scene_id`). The attachment endpoint recomputes the same list from the publication row itself (scene, the person on its route, session class): a caller cannot pass a scope, and deleting the configured edge returns the fence to own-scene-only without any code change.

An accepted attachment writes metadata only, on the first `SPEAK` row (`attachment`); bytes stay in the scoped blob store and travel over the attachment endpoint above. Delivered history then shows the picture as something she sent, with its media type and size, and says so differently when the adapter never declared image support, when the platform receipt's attachment evidence does not match the stored digest, or when the row was never delivered. Text-only messages keep their previous shape exactly: no attachment slot, no extra wording, in her context and in history search results.

## Schedules

The host uses DSH's native scheduler; it does not run a second host timer wheel. Scheduling, updating, or cancelling a plan is a character decision in the authorized scene. A due plan re-enters that scene and asks the character to decide what to do. A due event is not new authorization and does not mean its requested work has completed. Plan status and policy epoch are checked before dispatch. In a platform conversation (a group or a DM) the due plan is a core notice, not a platform input: it carries no channel envelope, so it is never re-checked as a member's message or shown in that conversation's session as one; it wakes the conversation as a scene tick, the plan's person must still be a member of the route (`SCHEDULE_ROUTE_REVOKED` otherwise), and what she says goes out on the conversation's own route, with no platform message to reply to.

Asuna uses DSH Web's Schedule service, waiting for it at start (a worker start fails, saying so, when it has not come up within a minute). With the `asuna-cognition-core` setting `mountSchedule` `false`, plans, heartbeat and settlement are off. By DSH design the `schedule_*` tools of the Schedule are visible to every root agent in the Host; Asuna's role and action presets restrict their own tools.

Her rhythms — the heartbeat (`presence`), nightly settlement and self-development — are plans the program owns: they never appear in her plan list, so her plan tool cannot cancel or retime them. The heartbeat runs at home, in the owner-private scene named by `persona_runtime.<persona>.heartbeat_target`, when her persona model also sets `heartbeat.enabled`; only the owner turns it on or off (a package may default `heartbeat.enabled`, never declare it as a policy key). Its pace is hers through core policy keys: `heartbeat.every_min` (15–240; anything outside is clamped), `heartbeat.min_gap_min` (0–720), and `heartbeat.pause_min` (0–720: beats skip until then and resume by themselves; 0 resumes now). Before any model call a beat is skipped, with one counting audit, when a role turn is running or queued at home (the action brain working on a task does not count), during her pause, within `min_gap_min` of her last internal turn at home (heartbeat, settlement or self-development) unless something reached her since — an inbound message that woke her in any routed scene, or a finished task — or after 24 beats that reached the model on that local day. A watchdog checks after every turn, at every settlement and self-development dispatch, and every five minutes from the host's existing watch thread: a heartbeat with no dispatch for two beats plus five minutes is audited as `rhythm.heartbeat_missed`, its native record replaced, and her next beat tells her it was reconnected. The five-minute check also recreates a native record deleted elsewhere and restores her pace on one retimed elsewhere (audited as `rhythm.retimed` with `drift`).

A heartbeat that passes its gates rolls a die first (`dice.py`): nothing (歇着), interesting (有趣), self development (自我开发), self evaluation (自我评估), review the dice (复盘) or reach out (找人), weighted by `heartbeat.dice_awake` or, inside her declared sleep window, `heartbeat.dice_asleep`. Both are her policy keys written with names (`歇着 40、有趣 31、自我开发 10、自我评估 12、复盘 2、找人 5` awake and `歇着 66、自我开发 20、自我评估 8、复盘 2、找人 4` asleep by default); a name left out weighs 0, `set_policy` refuses an unknown or repeated name and returns the whole stored table as `stored_table`. Self development counts as 0 where self-improvement is off, reach out where she has no peer line. The beat's occurrence seeds the roll, and `presence.rolled` records the outcome, the period and the weights. Nothing does not reach her (`SKIPPED:DICE_NOTHING`, no model call) unless waiting an hour would lose something only a heartbeat shows (someone on her waiting list, which ends after an hour; a peer line whose unseen asides already fill the six she is shown) or the owner knocked the beat by hand; whatever stays until she answers is shown at the next beat that reaches her. Her sleep window decides only which table rolls: a fully quiet night is `歇着 100`. Every other outcome opens her turn with one line naming the roll and the whole table; self development runs as a self-improvement turn (`self-development:dice:`); self evaluation is the heartbeat with `self_review_from_program` (`self_review.py`): her local day in raw readings with their sources (where she spoke and how often, tasks handed over and their state, refused calls by tool with the first refusal, tasks still open, `exchanges` and `cared`, below); review is the heartbeat with `dice_review_from_program`: every roll since her last review (at most 60 and 14 days) with whether it reached her and, for a beat that did, the tools it used, the lines it said and her reason when she stayed quiet, the count per outcome and both tables; reach out is the heartbeat naming her peer lines, to write a `note` to one if she wants. Only the program's own heartbeat event can carry a roll; the router drops it from anything else.

Her groups are active or resting (`focus.py`). An active group works as described here. In a resting group a line wakes her only for `mentioned_account` or `reply_to_character` (no gate) and, through the gate, `catchup_mention`, `awaited_answer`, `watched` or a notice that asks for a look (`poked`, `reacted`); any other reason is cleared at the channel intake and kept on the line as `group_context.resting`. A resting group has no proactive chance, its lines are kept and searchable but not summarized, and her turn there carries its lines of the last day (at most 40) instead of the last hour's. A group is active when its scene has `focus_active`, or when she is an owner or admin there (`people.self_role`); new groups rest. She chooses with `group_focus {op: list|active|rest, place}`: making a group active moves its `summary_start_seq` to now; resting an admin group is refused. The deployment setting `active_groups` (default 4) is the usual number of active groups: going past it works, and the result says what it costs and lists the active groups with their last day's lines. Her heartbeat's view of each group says whether it is active. A resting group's conversation title in the sidebar starts with `💤 ` (`focus.RESTING_MARK`); a change retitles it at once (the Host's `retitle` request).

Her switches (`switches.py`, collection `switches`) end by themselves: after `hours` (default 24, at most 168), or earlier with `set_policy {key: switch_off, value: <id>}`. At home, `group_focus {op: mute, place, hours}` mutes a group (`op: unmute` ends it): a muted group is not active whatever its focus (only the resting wakes, no proactive chance, no summaries), its visits are refused (`places.eligibility`), and her heartbeat lists it as muted. At home, `set_policy {key: tools_off, place, value: [tool names], hours}` turns tools off in one conversation (an empty list restores them; `think`, `recall` and `stay_silent` cannot be): they stay in that conversation's list, and a call is refused saying who turned it off, until when and the switch's id. Outside home, `understand_person {block: hours}` blocks the account speaking to her: the channel intake marks its lines (`event.blocked`, a group line's wake reason cleared and kept as `group_context.blocked`) and the router records them without a turn (`RECORDED_NO_WAKE`); nobody at home can be blocked. A switched conversation's turns carry `switches_from_program` with its own switches; her home turns carry all of them and those that ended in the last 12 hours.

A group's conversation is condensed on her choice, or by its size (`condense.py`). In a visit turn, at home in a
heartbeat, self-improvement or settlement turn, or in an owner-private turn a plan of hers woke, `group_focus {op:
condense, place}` marks a group (`condense_wanted` on its scene) and `op: keep` takes the mark back; any other turn
(a group, someone else's chat: someone there is waiting for her to speak) is refused. While she is in any group, a
program plan at home (`plan-asuna-condense`, every 5 minutes, not in her plan list) looks at each group at its quiet
spell: once someone other than her last spoke there long enough ago for how busy the group is (average incoming lines
a day over the last week: 100 or more 10 minutes, 30 to 100 15 minutes, fewer 30 minutes; her own lines never restart
the wait), or at once during the group's local night (01:00 to 07:00, its time zone). A marked group is condensed
then. Condensing asks the Host to run DSH's `/compact` on that group's main character session through the commands
service (`compact_session`; the session's log shows the command and its result), which the plugin refuses while a
turn of hers runs there; the next look tries again. A Host with no commands service or no `/compact` for that session
takes the mark back instead of trying again. A mark has no size minimum: `/compact` keeps the newest stretch of the conversation word for word
(DSH's retained tail) and summarizes the rest, so a conversation no longer than that stretch is left as it is
(`nothing_to_compact`) and the mark is cleared.

A group she has not marked (groups only: her private chats and home conversations keep her mark alone) is measured at
a quiet spell after new lines there: the Host request `conversation_size` answers the conversation's size in DSH's own
measure (the token meter its compaction threshold uses) and the floor, half of that conversation's compaction
threshold (the character preset's compaction engine, `compaction.js` `floorTokens`); the scene keeps both
(`conversation_size`). A reading under the floor stands until new lines arrive; a reading past it is taken again before
the program acts on it. After a condense the conversation is its retained tail and summary, under the floor, so a group is
condensed by size again only once it has grown past the floor again. Past the floor, the program first opens a notes visit there (a `visit` turn with intent
`condense`, not in her plan's visit ledger and outside its daily limit; `condense_floor` on the scene records it), whose
text and `visit_from_program` say that the program brought her to write the group's notes before its conversation is
condensed; it also carries the phrases a `write_notes` visit gets. At the next quiet spell after that turn has run,
the group is condensed; a visit that failed is offered again after an hour; a muted group waits. A summary request too
large for the model's window (the model refuses it for its context window) is made in steps: the first part of the
span, then that summary followed by the rest, halving again while a part does not fit, cut only before a user message.

A condensed group's scene gets `condensed_at` and `condensed_by` (`mark` or `floor`) and loses its mark. The first
turn of hers in that group afterwards carries `condensed_from_program` (that same turn again if it is prepared again;
the scene records it in `condensed_told`), saying the conversation was condensed, on her choice or past the floor
she agreed to, and that the summary at the start of that conversation is what is left. Each character turn's result
reports how long the conversation was on its latest request (`prompt_tokens`); the scene keeps it
(`conversation_tokens`), and a visit turn's `visit_from_program.upkeep.conversation` says it in words (短, 中等, 长,
很长), never as a number.

A `write_notes` visit also carries `visit_from_program.upkeep.phrases`: at most 5 phrases of that group (Chinese runs
of 2 to 6 characters, Latin words of 3 or more letters, markup, @s and links left out, and pieces made only of common
function characters) that at least 3 people other than her used there over the last week, on at least 3 different
days, at least once since her previous visit there, not used by 2 or more people in 2 or more of her other groups,
and not already in her group notes; most people first, a phrase overlapping one already shown left out, each with how
many people in words. What she makes of them goes into her group notes.

A heartbeat at home, or a plan of her own that comes due there, may send her on a visit. With `heartbeat.visits` on, her presence turn — and a `scheduled` turn in an owner-private conversation — carries `places_from_program`: each routed, unblocked group as words only — how busy it is and when the last line was, how many people spoke in the last hour, when she last spoke there, her last visit and what came of it, whether someone called her since she last spoke, her notes' headings, and whether she can go now, and, when she gave the group a time zone, how its clock stands to hers (`their_clock`: 那边的钟比你这边快／慢 so many hours, or the same; it changes only with a time zone or a daylight-saving switch) — plus `last_visits_from_program`. In a home turn `place_timezone` gives a group its time zone (`do` `set` with `place` and an IANA `timezone`, `clear`, or `list`: every group, its zone or 没设, and the time there; a set answers with the time there, so a wrong zone shows at once); it is stored on the scene (`timezone`), decides that group's night and is the time zone of plans she makes there; an unset group keeps the Host's clock. No line anyone wrote crosses. The `visit` tool (`place`, `intent` = `start_topic` | `share_picture` | `check_in` | `write_notes`, optional `topic` ≤ 80 characters and `artifact_id` of a picture she produced) is offered only there, up to `heartbeat.visits_per_beat` times a turn (1–3, default 1; hers to set, shown as `places_from_program.this_beat`; the cooldown still stops a second visit to the same group), and is refused with the reason when the group is not open to her now: today's `heartbeat.visits_per_day` (0–8, default 4) used up, night there (the route's `proactive` quiet hours, or 23:00–08:00, on the route's offset, else the group's own time zone, else the Host's clock), within `heartbeat.after_own_min` (30–1440, default 360; doubled when what she last said there got no answer) of her last speech or visit there (a visit whose turn there has not run yet is refused as 已经出门去那个群了，那边那一回合还没跑完, and its outcome reads 那边那一回合还没跑完，结果还没出来), or — for `start_topic` and `share_picture` only — someone spoke within `heartbeat.quiet_min` (0–240, default 30); going to look or take notes while people talk is fine. A visit whose input failed never got there: it comes home as such and spends neither her day nor the group's wait. An accepted visit queues an internal `visit` input in that group (`visit:<scene>:<beat event>`, wake reason `visit`). It carries no channel envelope — it is her own moment, not a platform message, so the group's session never shows a member saying it — and wakes as a scene tick; what she says goes out on the group's own route. the turn is `public` because the scene is, titled "Her own visit", and sees that group's catch-up, her group notes, her produced pictures and `visit_from_program` (why she came, her topic, the room in words, whether anyone called her) — no relationship, speaker, rhythm, cross-scene experience or home places. It has no `visit` tool and no development grant. What she says there counts toward the per-group caps of unprompted speaking (`proactive`).

A chain calls her in any of her groups, whether or not unprompted speaking (`proactive`) is enabled there: when the newest line ends a run of the same text — or the same picture or sticker, by file, or store sticker, by id — from at least three different people, with no line of hers inside it, the line is woken with reason `chain` (`group_context.chain` = what, how many people, whether it is media). Only the night quiet hours (the route's `proactive` block even when it is off, else 23:00–08:00) and a busy foreground hold it; the burst, cooldown and hourly gates do not, because a chain is fast. One chain calls her once (`proactive.chain.woke` on the line); her own line ends the run. The relevance gate asks 接话 or 不理 with the reason in words, and her turn carries `chain_from_program` (what, how many people, how to send the same thing: copy the line, or keep the sticker by its ref and send `[表情包:名字]`). Whether and how she joins is hers.

## Managed integration and self-development

The managed integration runner is granted by the scene: the configured local owner profile carries every integration tool, while a task delegated from a home round woken on an agent line carries the narrow `agent_home` grant — `integration_start` and `integration_status` only, no test run, no stop, no import. The adapter's code lives in its channel package and changes only through the development tools. `integration_test` runs a frozen, read-only `/app` copy of the development candidate's adapter with separate writable `/data`; `integration_start` enables a frozen copy of the published adapter as a managed process, and host restart restores the then-published adapter. Unpublished edits never run as the managed service. `integration_status` reports process state and bounded logs, not platform connectivity or delivery. `integration_stop` stops the managed process and disables host restart restoration.

A started service (`integration_start`) is restored on every start; `integration_stop` keeps it stopped. An enabled integration whose adapter serves a channel (`adapter_config.host.channel_id`) starts that channel's resident command (its kind's `SERVICE_ARGV`) from the published adapter by itself, when it was never started or stopped before; a failure shows in `integration_status`. Integration processes run under the Host sandbox like any command: they may write only their data folder, and reach the configured endpoints directly at each device's own address (the adapter settings may still name an endpoint by its configured local `port`; the run's settings carry the device's address instead). Their commands are argument arrays, not host-shell strings; `/app`, `/data` and `/integration/config.json` in an argument name the run's frozen adapter copy, its data folder and its settings (also `$ASUNA_INTEGRATION_CONFIG` and `$ASUNA_INTEGRATION_DATA`). Connection credentials come from the local integration profile and are not copied into ordinary task files. A test run (`integration_test`, unpublished adapter code) uses the real settings and can act on a platform for real (`integration_test` belongs to the owner profile alone); messages are meant to leave through the outbox and the published adapter started with `integration_start`. Each run's copy of the adapter is removed when it ends; only the service's copy is kept. An endpoint marked `"tls": true` is reached over TLS without verifying the device's certificate; `"read_only"` is a description of the device, not enforced.

`import_integration_artifact` reads one artifact over one of those configured endpoint aliases: the model supplies the alias and a path, never a URL, host or port, and the fetch performs a single HTTP/1.1 GET without following redirects. The host writes the bytes into the task workspace bound to this action at a relative path — existing files are kept unless `overwrite=true`, protected inputs are never written through, and the size cap is 4 MiB (1 MiB by default). The result reports the relative path, absolute path, byte count and SHA-256 actually written, or the real failure: unknown endpoint, URL rejected, path outside the workspace, target exists, over limit, HTTP status or transport error. When the bytes actually written are a PNG/JPEG/WebP/GIF by file signature (not by file name or `Content-Type`), the host additionally registers them as an `image` artifact in the scope this task is bound to — never a scope the model names: `kind=image`, `storage=gridfs`, `state=DONE`, media type from the bytes, and a SHA-256 the host recomputes and re-reads itself, with the result then carrying an `artifact` block (`artifact_id`, digest, size) so a later turn can send that picture out. Bytes that are not an image change nothing: the result keeps exactly its previous field set and no artifact row is written. A registration that cannot happen (a scope the store does not know, bytes over the 8 MiB outbound cap, a storage error) never turns a completed import into a failure — the reason is reported honestly under `artifact`. Offline: `python3 tools/integration_import_offline_check.py` and `python3 tools/outbound_image_offline_check.py`.

`generate_image` draws one picture with the endpoint aliased `image`, for any task: it is not one of the integration tools (those belong to the owner profile, or to the narrow agent-line grant, which carries only `integration_start` and `integration_status`), and this tool is the one fixed sequence of that service's agent API (`project-comfyui-agent/v1`). The model supplies a prompt and a few choices — an optional ready `workflow` id (otherwise `POST /project/resolve` picks one: text-to-image with `content_mode=sfw`, optionally for a `style`, or `input_mode=image-edit` when there is a source picture), `negative_prompt`, `width`/`height` (64–2048, multiples of 8), `seed`, and `options` limited to `steps`, `cfg`, `sampler_name` and `scheduler` that the workflow's `parameter_schema` declares — never a request, URL or path. The host runs `integration_image.py` as a trusted one-shot integration run that knows only the `image` endpoint and carries no adapter settings: it checks the workflow, `POST /project/generate` with `batch_size` 1, polls `GET /project/jobs/{prompt_id}` and fetches the first `/view` output (at most 8 MiB). The runner lock covers only the launch, so integration tools, status and stop do not wait behind a generation. A job still queued or running when `timeout` (default 180 s, at most 300) runs out is reported with its `prompt_id`; calling again with `job` collects it without drawing again. The host verifies the byte count and SHA-256, writes the picture into the task workspace (a new file under `images/`, or `target_relative_path`; never over an existing file) and registers it in the task's own scope with source `integration:image:<view path>`, so it counts as her own picture. The registered copy, the one that can be sent, has its PNG text chunks removed (the service writes its whole request there); the workspace file keeps them. The service's refusal (HTTP status and its `error` object) is returned as it was. `workflows=true` draws nothing: it lists the ready workflows that draw from text or work from a picture (`source_image`: `required` or `optional`, from the workflow's `parameter_schema` `input_image`) (a workflow that also requires another picture, such as an inpaint `mask_image`, is not listed and is refused by name: `IMAGE_WORKFLOW_NEEDS_MORE_PICTURES`) with the service's own guidance for each (prompt style and structure, quality prefix, best for and not for, recommended size, an example prompt), and every drawn result carries the same guidance for the workflow that drew it. Without a named `workflow` the service picks a generic anime one, and some workflows (such as Qwen-Image) are chosen only when named, so the brief says to list first. Nobody has seen a drawn picture until a model looks: the result and the brief say to call `read_image` with its `artifact_id`, compare it with what was asked and draw again when it is wrong. The task brief names this tool first for pictures; public generators reachable by `web_fetch` stay allowed, but their pictures cannot enter the workspace and can only be linked. `source_image` edits a picture: its ref or `artifact_id`, read with `read_image`'s own fence for the task's conversation (no route check: the bytes go to the service, not to a model). The bytes travel beside the script in its frozen copy; the script uploads them (`POST /upload/image`, named `asuna-edit-<sha>-<random>`), passes the name as the workflow's `input_image`, and deletes the upload (`DELETE /project/inputs/{name}`) when the job is over; a job still queued keeps it, and any later call removes this client's uploads older than an hour. A named workflow that takes no picture is refused with a source (`IMAGE_WORKFLOW_TAKES_NO_SOURCE`), one that needs a picture without one (`IMAGE_WORKFLOW_NEEDS_SOURCE`); a source the task may not read is `SOURCE_IMAGE_NOT_READABLE` with the reason. The service fits an oversized source to the route and uses the first frame of an animated one; each of its warnings comes back with its code, message and the original and used size. An edit of a picture she drew herself is hers like any drawing; an edit of anyone else's picture is registered in the task's scope with source `edit:<scope>|integration:image:<view path>`, is not hers, and may be sent only back to the conversation the picture came from (`outbound_media.returns_to`), where the turn's `image_artifacts_from_program` lists it first, marked `edited_from_here`; the owner's private chats still take what their own scopes hold. Offline: `tests/test_image_generation.py`, `tests/test_image_edit_provenance.py`.

`private_words` (both brains, every conversation and task, except a turn opened by a note from outside) keeps the deployment's list of words that point at real people and places (`private_words.py`): `do` `add` (`category` person, group, account, site, host, email or other; `word`; `why`, one line of at most 200 characters; the same word again replaces its entry), `remove` (`word`, exactly) or `summary` (entries per category). Outside home (a conversation or task that is not owner-private) it only adds: a word already listed keeps its entry, the answer is the same whether or not it was listed (`kept`, `category`), and `remove` is refused. The list is `<data>/private/personal-words.txt` (`ASUNA_PRIVATE_WORDS` names another file), one `category|word|why` per line. An entry of Latin letters or digits only needs at least 3 characters and matches as a whole word, case-insensitive; other entries need at least 2 characters and match where they appear. Nothing returns or prints a listed word: results and hits name the category and the entry's line. `tests/test_personal_words.py` refuses a committed file containing an entry and is skipped, with the path, where there is no list; a persona skill's own check may read the same file.

`render_svg` turns an SVG file in the task folder into a PNG, for any task, in every conversation: it runs none of the model's code and reads only the task folder. The worker (`svg_render.py`) reads the file (UTF-8, at most 2 MiB), refuses one that declares XML entities, and removes the link of every `<image>` or `<feImage>` that is not an embedded `data:` picture (resvg would otherwise load a picture named by an absolute path on the Host), saying how many it removed. The Host renders it with resvg (`@resvg/resvg-js`, `packages/cognition-core/src/svg.js`) in a worker thread, with the system fonts (and the font folders `/usr/share/fonts`, `/usr/local/share/fonts`, which a Linux image without fontconfig needs), an optional `width` (16–2048, aspect kept) and `background` colour, at most 2048×4096 pixels and 20 s. The PNG is written to a new file under `images/` and registered in the task's scope with source `integration:svg:<path>`, so it counts as her own picture: `read_image` takes its `artifact_id` and it can be sent. Offline: `tests/test_svg_render.py`, `packages/cognition-core/test/svg.test.js`.

Self-development uses persistent project candidates separate from ordinary task workspaces. The default target is the selected persona package; `project="core"` selects the authorized cognition source. Tools inspect bounded file pages, edit/run candidate files, read bounded records from the existing database, and publish frozen artifacts after a non-consuming boot probe. A publication's files are written into the project's source and committed there under the character's git name (`character_git`; without it, the checkout's own identity): only those paths, so other staged work stays as it is, and nothing when git ignores the source. A commit that fails is reported in the receipt (`commit_error`) and does not undo the publication. The core package holds no `docs/` (it does carry this file, as `python/asuna/resources/RUNTIME_API.md`): a publication that changes only files under `docs/` leaves the package's hash unchanged and lands only as that commit. A package byte for byte the one running (a change outside its files only) is active at once and restarts nothing. Persona resources activate without restarting DSH. Python code replaces the worker at an idle boundary; JS/composition/dependency changes return `HOST_RESTART_REQUIRED`. Until the Host restarts, a self-improvement turn cannot publish to that project again (`DEVELOPMENT_PUBLISH_WAITING_RESTART`). Failed candidates remain for forward correction through the independent native recovery preset. The development tools come only with work handed over in an owner-private conversation (her local chat, the owner's DM, the peer line or an agent line) in a turn woken by someone's words there, her own self-improvement turn, a plan of hers coming due there, or a note from another of her trusted home conversations; any other scene's request can only become an entry in her idea notebook (`ideas` note; the action brain's `note_idea`), read and decided only in her self-improvement turns or a private chat with the owner (`ideas` read, review; `review` takes the idea's `id`). A self-improvement turn lists the first 20 undecided ideas (open first, then deferred, oldest first); `ideas` read takes `page` and `search` (≤ 40 characters, matched in the idea or its reason) so she can reach the rest. The memory tab's owner-private view lists the notebook and her decisions.
