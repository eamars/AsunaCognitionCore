compression happens when she picks what to carry. The STORED memory is left as it is and keeps growing: there is no

## 1. The answers in short

1. **The design after a month.** Every store has a full life cycle on paper and in code. In practice the program's
   forgetting does not run: of 229 moves since 10-13, her own `let_go` made 191, the program's stay lapse fired **once**
   (11-01), cooling, supersession, merge and the skipped-gist path fired **zero** times. What limits what she carries
   is the ADR-096 token ceiling: home sits at 12,278 of 12,288 tokens and the gate refused 125 writes in 22 days, the
   exact failure sign ADR-062 §7 named ("the cap is still doing the forgetting"). The carried side therefore has four
   overlapping mechanisms (count caps, the token ceiling, her let_go, the stay lapse) where one would do.
   Efficiency: a home turn carries about 50k characters of context, of which her identity layer is 8.3k, her
   understanding of the person 5.8k, memories 5.2k; at the model 124k tokens a request, 94.6% read from cache.
   Recall by heading works (86 reads in 22 days) but `recall` takes 2.8–4.9 s at the median and her use of it fell from
   138 a week to about 40.
2. **Phase 3.** *Understanding as items:* build it, scoped to a per-person document with a carried cap; the owner's
   own person record is at 3,563 of 4,000 characters and grows 194 characters a rewrite, the `current_self` failure of
   §1 about to repeat on the one record read 600 times a day. *The idea age word:* drop it; no open idea is older than
   2.4 days, and the deferred pile (323) already shows its dates. *Sticker last-used words:* already built
   (`stickers.review_block` says 收了以后还没发过 and 上次发是…); mark done.
3. **Bugs.** Eleven, §5. The three that matter: heartbeat template text drives the same three journal rows into
   every heartbeat and counts them as memorable across conversations (the loop §4.1 meant to exclude); the
   `correction` op, the only supersession path, has never succeeded (41 attempts, 41 refusals: it is allowed only on
   ledgers, and no ledger exists); every turn re-reads every journal row, every document and every tier row
   (about 100–150 ms today, linear in the store).
4. **Storage.** Measured, near-duplicates in her stores are small: 0 persona pairs at 0.93, 2 of 40 self items,
   2 of 208 group-note sections, 32 of 1,098 journal rows (3%, written 0.9 days apart). In retrieval, 15% of turns
   return two units sharing a source message and 6% return two halves of one message; superseded versions: none
   returned, because she rewrites in place (611 replaces) and the correction path never worked. The waste is real but

   tree+blob model (content-addressed sections, snapshots that share unchanged parts), not deltas; and the heads model
   is already right where it exists. Recommendation: do not build a delta store; move the one working document she

5. **The path forward.** The stores scale (1 GB a year of revisions, 3 GB a year of chat vectors; MongoDB is fine
   with that). What does not scale is per-turn work that walks the whole store, the lexical recall pool capped at the
   newest 4,096 units (saturated in 95% of turns already), and recall latency. With the four fixes in §6.5 the design
   carries her for years; without them the worker's per-turn cost grows about 10× in a year.

## 4. Grounding that changes a decision

- **Two strengths (Bjork & Bjork 1992):** storage strength never falls; retrieval strength does. The owner's rule is
  this theory applied correctly: the store keeps everything, availability is decided at carry and recall time. Every
  mechanism that edits the store to imitate fading (gist replacing the head text, merge as a duty, cooling as a
  state) is outside it.
- **Encoding specificity (Tulving & Thomson 1973) and context-dependent memory (Godden & Baddeley 1975):** what comes
  back depends on the cue present at retrieval. A person's boundary comes back when the person is the cue. That is why
  the person layer travels with the person (ADR-082), and why a boundary kept anywhere else (a journal row, a group
  note) has no cue. The guarantee is structural: it holds exactly where the record is carried with the person.
- **Reconsolidation (Nader et al. 2000; Lee 2009):** a memory is updated when it is active. For her, "active" is the
  moment she writes. A duplicate noticed at write time can be merged by the author then; the same pair offered at
  night, a day later, is a chore (0 merges in 22 nights, §3.3).
- **Information retrieval on versioned text:** search engines index the current version of a page and keep history
  apart; near-duplicate detection is done at index time by shingling or hashing (Broder 1997; Charikar 2002), not by
  storing deltas. Her item index already indexes current sections only, and her near-duplicate rate is 0–3% (§3.6).
- **Git (Chacon & Straub, Pro Git, ch. 10):** objects are content-addressed and immutable (identical content stored
  once); a commit names a tree and its parents (history, nothing overwritten); a ref is a pointer to one commit (what
  is current is a lookup, not a search); packfiles store deltas as an on-disk encoding under snapshot semantics ("git
  stores snapshots, not differences"). Three of the four already hold here (immutable revisions with parents, heads).
  The fourth, sharing unchanged content, is what the revision ledger lacks: a revision copies the whole document.
  Deltas are git's storage optimisation, not its model; copying git's model means content-addressed sections, not a
  delta chain.
- **Lexical recall in a bounded pool:** BM25-style recall over a fixed-size recent window reaches only that window.
  Her pool is the newest 4,096 units of a scope (§3.7), saturated in 95% of turns: older text is reachable by vector
  only, which ADR-097 §2 showed misses exact words. This is a scaling limit that exists today, not in a year.

## 5. Findings (each with its broken invariant)

**B1. Heartbeat template text counts as use and as a memorable moment across conversations.** Invariant (§4.1):
"one conversation echoing itself counts nothing". Nine fixed heartbeat texts drive retrieval; the same three journal
rows came up in 28 of 29 heartbeats and in 24 of them two at once (§3.5). Each surfacing writes `last_use_at` (the row
never cools) and, because the home conversation and the group scheduled turns are different conversations, the second
place is a memorable moment: 114 `surfaced_again` moments, 76 of 82 几处都提到 offers point at journal rows. The pilot's
own rule (none in heartbeats) is violated in production. Fix class: a turn whose query is program text (heartbeat,
scheduled, visit) surfaces but counts nothing; `deliver` already knows the turn kind.

**B2. The correction op has never worked.** Invariant (§4.2 step 5, §6): supersession is the way a fact is replaced
and the old revision kept dated. `documents.problems` allows `correction` only on a ledger; no ledger exists; she tried
41 times (work log and others), all refused; `superseded` moves 0. The design's correctness answer to "which is
current" has no working path. Fix class: either allow correction wherever `replace_section` is allowed, or drop the op
and let the revision ledger plus 「已被更正」 (crossing.before) be the answer (§6.4 recommends the drop).

**B3. Every turn re-reads the whole store.** `Tiers.sync` reads every document's current revision and every journal
row with its body (1,098 rows, 3.6 MB) to check for rows it has not seen; `tick`, `things`, `deliver` and `overflows`
each scan every tier row (no index but `_id`). Timed 12:10 NZDT: 43 + 15 + 21 ms per scan, three to four scans a
turn, about 100–150 ms of worker CPU under the interpreter lock, linear in the store. At the observed growth the
same work is 1–1.5 s a turn in a year. Fix class: sync on write (a new section gets its row in `written`, where every
write already passes), an index on (row, persona, place, store), counts kept per store rather than recounted.

**B4. The identity ceiling refuses instead of lapsing.** Invariant (§4.2 step 3): "adding a 13th does not refuse: the
least recently memorable lapses". The count caps (self 12, persona 18, notes 10, voice 6) never bind in tokens; the
ADR-096 ceiling binds first and refuses (125 refusals in 22 days, home at 12,278 / 12,288). The flip the ADR was built
for (the newest stays, the stalest lapses) is undone by the later gate. Fix class: one ceiling (tokens per pair), one
overflow rule (her choice for a turn, then the least recently memorable lapses), no refusal; the count caps go.

**B5. The legacy `current_self` lines are still indexed.** `memory_pilot.own_items` embeds the 16 list lines of the
old body (84 characters now) beside the 40 `self` sections; they have no tier row, never deliver, and one of the two
self pairs at 0.95 is such a line against its own successor. Fix: stop indexing `current_self` once `self` exists.

**B6. Recall takes 2.8–4.9 s at the median** (§3.4), and her recalls fell from 138 a week to about 40. The path does a
full retrieval (one embedding call, a vector search over 193k units, the lexical pool), her thoughts elsewhere (a second
embedding call), the cross-place word index top-up, past versions, and the headings. Which part costs what is not
recorded. Fix class: one profile reading of a recall's parts (tool_watch has the whole; the parts need a line each),
then bound the slow one; a candidate is the top-up of `crossing_words` (33,536 rows) at each recall.

**B7. `history()` is a collection scan.** `state_revisions` has no index on `entity_key`; `crossing.versions` runs the
query on every recall with a query, per readable slug, even when its cache is warm (the seal check itself is the
scan). 10 ms today because the 113 MB fit in cache; it grows with every revision. Fix: an index on
(entity_key, created_at), one line in the catalog.

**B8. The chunker races with the Host on message rows.** 314 `state.conflict` audits since 10-14 on `messages`
(STALE_REVISION, DUPLICATE_ID) from the `chunk` stream: `MemoryService.chunk` marks each message row
(`memory_chunk_version`) with the revision it read, while receipts land on the same row. The indexer catches the
conflict, records it once and doubles its pause up to 5 minutes, so that scene's new lines wait for the next round.
No text is lost. Fix class: keep the chunk watermark per scene (a scene field or a `chunked_to` row), never on the
message row.

**B9. Her `budget_per_place.py` crashes** at `memory_tiers.py:239` (`store.collection`): the skill passes a shim with
only `db` into `memory_tiers.placed`, whose store interface changed with ADR-104. Reproduced on a copy 12:00 NZDT. The
skill is hers; the fix is three lines (it already builds a real `Store` in `store_for_limit()`; pass that). Class: her
skills reach program internals without a contract; `tools/carried_check.py`'s `ReadOnly` store is the supported way.

**B10. The night list is about the work log.** 104 of 128 non-weekly offers point at journal rows, which are memories
she may ignore; the merge path (mandatory for facts) has 8 group-note offers and 0 merges. The list costs a settlement
turn its space (about 2,500 characters) for a yield of 17 acts on 82 repeated offers.

**B11. Two overlapping growth walls remain.** The 1 MiB revision cap and the whole-document copy were fixed for
`work_log` only (phase 0); working document #12 (125 sections, 320 KB a snapshot, 145 revisions in 18 days) is the
same shape and reaches the wall at about 400 sections, in roughly two months at its pace. Class: any document she
appends to as a log.

Smaller: `understand_person` refuses 40% of calls, 23 of them "read it first" (ADR-082's write rule), each costing her a
turn; the person-layer body cap (4,000) will refuse the owner's record after about two more rewrites (3,563, +194 a
rewrite); 4,636 pilot turn rows from 10-13..10-25 wait for the 30-day prune (expected, 11-24).

**Efficiency**, per conversation kind (§3.5): the identity layer is 8.3k characters at home and 6.1–6.5k in groups
(the ADR designed 2,400 for `self` alone; `self` plus `core` is 3.5× that); the understanding block 5.8k at home;
memories 5.2k; `things_from_program` 164. The night job is one settlement turn a day at about 51.6k characters plus
the word-index rebuild. Index growth: about 350 items a week, 18k a year, 110 MB of vectors a year. Recall hit rates:
own item in 6.6% of queried recalls; a surfaced own item in 78% of home turns, 11% of group turns.

**Verdict.** The design completes the life cycle on paper; in operation the carried side is run by her discipline and
the ADR-096 gate, not by memorability. That is not a failure of the rule; it is four mechanisms where the rule needs
one. The stored side's imitation of forgetting (gist in the head text, cooling as a state, mandatory merge, supersession
by a special op) has produced 6 gists, 0 coolings, 0 merges, 0 supersessions in 22 days and can go (§6.6).

### 6.2 Phase 3 items against her pattern

**Understanding layers as items.** Her pattern: 7 person records, rewritten whole (40 revisions), growing 194
characters a rewrite; the one she rewrites most (20 revisions) is at 3,563 of 4,000 characters and is read in every
home turn (599 of 939 turns carried both layers, 5.8k characters). The 4,000 cap will refuse it within about two
rewrites and she will compress into the space: the §1 `current_self` failure on the one record that matters most. In
groups she already carries a one-line short (74% of entries), which is the compression at carry time the rule asks
for. Options:
- A. **A document per person, sections as items, a carried cap per person** (the phase-3 plan): `understand_person`
  keeps its name and its read-first rule; the record becomes sections she writes; the tiers carry up to N per person
  and the rest is at hand (by meaning, by key, by `recall person`); the short stays as one fixed section. The one-time
  split is hers, as it was for `self` (no conversion). Cost: ADR-082's revision pins move onto document base revisions
  (the same CAS); the entry and `person_view` read sections; about the size of the `self` split. Her choice of what
  stays carried is the compression; a boundary or a promise is carried while she keeps it carried, and lapses
  only by her let_go (facts never cool): the guarantee becomes "what she chose", measured by the carried section
  count per person.
- B. Keep one body and raise the cap. Cost: the carried block grows without bound in every 1:1 turn; no compression.
- C. A carried part beside the body (a long short). Simpler than A but a second place to keep in step with the body.

Recommendation: **A**, built after she has read it, measured first on the owner's record. Under A the hard case has a
plain answer: in 1:1 the person's carried items are in front of her every turn (as the whole body is today); in a
group only the woken person's short is, as today, and a boundary she wants in front of her in groups goes in the
short. Today's guarantee, measured: every one-to-one turn with a person carried both layers (599 of 599; the 72
note-woken turns show no person); group entries with a person short 74%, with neither layer 16%.

**The idea age word 很久没动.** Her pattern: an open idea is decided within a day (9 open, all under 2.4 days); the
pile is `deferred` (323, median 5.3 days since the last decision, max 22.5), where each entry already shows its date

**Sticker last-used words.** `stickers.review_block` already says 收了以后还没发过 and 上次发是 + a rough-ago tier,

### 6.4 The storage model

**Measured.** Near-duplicates among current items (the only ones indexed): persona 0 pairs at 0.93 of 58 sections, self
2 of 40, group notes 2 of 208, core 1 of 13, journal 32 of 1,098 (the same day's work written again: 0.9 days apart at
the median); across stores 5 pairs (persona×self 3). Nothing at 0.97. Identical bodies under different ids: 0. The
night list has offered 14 distinct pairs; she merged none by the designed path. In retrieval: two units from one
source message in 15% of turns, two halves of one message in 6%, a superseded version 0 (nothing is superseded),
a corrected unit 3 of 3,358. "An item and its revisions all indexed": does not happen; the index holds current
sections only and marks removed ones `gone`.

So the retrieval problem the owner suspects is small on her stores today. The space problem is real but is not
near-duplicates: it is whole-document copies (113 MB for 1.03 MB live; one working log at 320 KB a write).

**What already follows git.** Heads: one `state_heads` row per entity and scope naming its current revision; "which
is current" is a lookup for every document and understanding record. Revisions with parents: `state_revisions` rows
name `parent_revision_id`, nothing is overwritten, 0 dangling parents, 0 heads pointing at missing revisions. Journal
rows: per-entry revisions, 2.4× the live text, the right shape. Identity: a section's `sid` is the item's identity;
she keeps it by rewriting in place (611 replaces against 1,229 appends).

**What does not.** Sharing unchanged content: a document revision stores every section again (persona: 1 changed, 58
copied, 240 times). Supersession: designed as `correction`, never usable (B2). Write-time identity: none; the program
tells her of a near-duplicate a night later.

**Options.**
- A. **Deltas** (a chain of diffs per document, snapshots every N): saves most of the 113 MB; changes nothing for
  retrieval; makes every history read a replay; a new encoding the rest of the code must learn. Cost high, benefit
  disk only.
- B. **Content-addressed sections** (git's tree+blob): a revision is an ordered list of (sid, body hash); bodies stored
  once by hash; a read of a document is one query by hash list; history reads stay simple. Saves the same disk with
  snapshot semantics. Cost: `documents._commit`, `DocumentStore.read`, `history()`'s four readers, `detail()`; old
  revisions stay as they are (not wrong, only fat), which leaves two shapes to read: a compatibility path the
  discipline dislikes. Benefit disk only, again.
- C. **Rows for logs, nothing else** (the phase-0 fix applied to its class): any document she appends to as a running
  record is `kind: journal`; she re-creates #12 as one (hers, one-time, as with `work_log`). Removes the 320 KB copies
  and the 1 MiB wall where they are. Cost: her one re-creation; a line in `write_document`'s result when a working
  document passes, say, 30 sections, naming the kind. Everything else stays.
- D. **Write-time identity line:** when an append in a store is at 0.93 or above to an existing item there, the write
  result says so and names the item's ref (no refusal, no merge); the night-time merge offer goes. One vector
  comparison against the in-memory index (about 1 ms) and one embedding of the new body (the same call the index
  makes within ten minutes anyway, moved to the write). Identity stays hers: she replaces, or keeps both.

**Recommendation: C now, D with the night-list simplification (§6.6), not A, not B.** Deltas and content-addressing
save disk the deployment does not lack (20 MB a week) and fix no retrieval problem the records show. If the copy
amplification ever matters, B is the git lesson to take, never A. On who decides identity at write time: her, with the
program's one line (D); automatic merging would be the program rewriting her items, which ADR-043 refused.

### 6.5 The path forward

Projected from the observed rates (§3.6): in a year the revision ledger is about 1 GB (0.1 GB with C), chat memory
units about 700k with 3 GB of vectors, the item index 18k items, journal rows 15k, group-note sections 2k, tier rows
20k. MongoDB and its vector index carry that. What breaks first, in order:

1. **Per-turn walks of the store (B3):** 10× the work in a year, in the worker's reply path. Must become incremental.
2. **The lexical recall pool (§3.7, §4):** capped at the newest 4,096 units and already saturated; a word said three
   days ago in a busy group is reachable by vector only. Not a growth problem but a ceiling reached. Options: a
   per-scope lexical index (the `crossing_words` shape already exists for cross-place words; the same index for the
   scope's own lines would replace the 4,096 window), or raise the window with a bound in bytes.
3. **Recall latency (B6):** at 3–5 s she uses recall less (138 → 40 a week); the catalogue path depends on her using
   it.
4. **The carried side (B4, B1):** bounded by the ceiling, so it does not grow, but the pressure does (refusals), and
   the echo keeps three rows in two of six slots.

With those four the design supports years; the store itself was never the limit. What the owner's rule removes
(§6.6) also removes most of the night list's cost and the gist and merge bookkeeping.
